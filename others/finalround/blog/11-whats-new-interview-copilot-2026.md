## Key takeaways

- **One product, one loop.** Interview CoPilot™ is the product; live help, practice, debriefs, screen help, and stealth are capabilities inside it. The mental model is **Prepare → Perform or Practice → Review**, repeated for every job you are chasing.
- **Everything starts from a Goal.** A Goal is one specific job—role, company, job description, your resume and materials, practice runs, and debriefs all attach to it.
- **Preflight is the new launch ritual.** Go Live and Practice both open a Preflight check first; the session starts only when you press Start.
- **Coding and system design now run on screen help.** No mode toggling—Auto-capture, Solve screen, and a Results panel handle technical questions.
- **Plans changed.** There is no free trial anymore; live sessions require an active paid plan.

## The short version: one desktop app, not many web tools

Dana's last job search, two years ago, looked nothing like this. There was a copilot here, a mock interview there, a career coach somewhere else—each a separate product with a separate button and a separate login. She remembers the night before a big call, copy-pasting her STAR stories out of one app so she could see them while another one ran. That is the chaos this update kills. **Interview CoPilot™ is now the desktop app experience**—live assistance during interviews, practice against an AI interviewer, automatic debriefs, and screen help all under one roof. The web tools and the older desktop flow run on a different engine and may behave differently; the new docs and the new experience live in the desktop app.

This matters because the most fragile part of interview prep has never been finding one tip. It is keeping a whole loop together when your calendar gets real. A candidate who preps in one tool, gets help in another, and reviews in a third is really doing three jobs, not one. The update collapses them into a single surface Dana downloads once and opens every time she touches her job search.

## The loop: Prepare → Perform or Practice → Review

The new product is built around one repeating pattern, and Dana can feel it in her own week:

1. **Prepare.** She creates a Goal for the exact role and company. Adds the job description, uploads her resume and materials.
2. **Perform or Practice.** She goes live in a real interview, or rehearses out loud with an AI interviewer using the same Goal context.
3. **Review.** She reads the automatic debrief from each session and uses it to sharpen the next round.

None of these steps is optional decoration. A Goal without a debrief is a one-way ticket; a debrief without a Goal has nothing to attach to. The compounding happens when the loop repeats: each practice run and each real session feeds the next prep round, so Dana's second interview for this job starts ahead of where the first one ended. That is the difference between practicing and building a campaign.

## Every interview starts from a Goal

On Tuesday night, Dana stops treating the app as a guessing game. Before this update, "getting ready" meant logging in and hoping the tool inferred what she wanted from thin air. The effect is subtle but important: **the copilot's suggestions stop being generic**. Answers are shaped by her resume, the job description, her materials, and the Goal context—not by a question bank that treats every candidate as interchangeable. Dana notices it in the practice room, where follow-ups sound like they were written for a staff-level systems role, because she told the app that is what the job is. ## Preflight: the calm before you go live

Thursday night, Dana rehearses the last sixty seconds before a call. Her instinct, inherited from the old product, is that clicking "Go Live" should start the session instantly. It does not anymore, and that is a feature, not a delay. **Go Live and Practice both open Preflight first**—an in-window check that shows what the session is about to use. She confirms the Goal setup and, for this session only, tweaks which resume, materials, answer language, and code language Copilot will lean on. The live session begins only when she presses **Start session**.

The old launch window, the audio meters, and the "Listen Check" step are gone. Permission and device checks now live in onboarding and Settings, so Preflight stays focused on what is actually useful: verifying the setup *before* the moment that matters. If you are the kind of candidate who sweats the last sixty seconds, think of Preflight as the part where the product holds still for you. Dana's breathing slows down in that window—everything is checked, nothing left to fiddle with, just the Start button and the phone number on the invite.

## Coding and system design: help lives on the screen

Friday morning, the technical round. Dana's loop includes coding, and the biggest change is that **there is no coding mode and no system-design mode to switch into**. Copilot runs on one engine and decides, per question, what it needs. When a technical question needs visual context, it reads her screen.

Screen help works in three pieces:

- **Auto-capture**—on by default, reads the screen when it is useful.
- **Solve screen**—a user-triggered screen read when you want it explicitly.
- **Results panel**—full written coding and system-design solutions shown beside the answer feed, and saved into the debrief afterward.

The question appears on screen—a whiteboard-style problem over video. Auto-capture picks up the problem statement before Dana even finishes reading it aloud. Her spoken answer stays short and clean while the detailed approach, complexity notes, and edge cases land in the Results panel, where she can reference them without rambling. No toggling, no "which mode am I in" panic, just the problem, the answer, and her voice staying calm. The old "Scan Code" button is retired. ## Phone screens: tell Copilot which device is the interviewer

Later that week, a phone screen. Phone interviews used to be a vague promise in the marketing copy. They are now an explicit flow. In Preflight, Dana chooses between **"This computer"** and **"Another device"**—the second option is for when the interview happens on a phone or a second laptop. She records a **one-time voice sample** so Copilot can tell her voice from the interviewer's when both come through the same microphone.

A couple of honest notes, because setup should not oversell: the app does not promise perfect speaker separation in a noisy room. Labels can briefly correct themselves, and the voice sample works best when the phone is on speaker near the computer microphone in a quiet room. Dana clears the kitchen table, puts the phone on speaker, and runs a one-minute test call to a friend. Treat those as the environment conditions, not bugs. ## Stealth Mode is on by default

If you share your screen, the thing you are probably most worried about is the floating assistant being seen. **Stealth Mode hides the floating Copilot windows from Zoom, Meet, and Teams screen shares and recordings—and it is on by default.** Dana checks it once before Friday, during a private screen share with a friend, because there is not yet an in-app verification badge telling her "you are invisible right now." The one-time test costs her five minutes and removes the last source of nerves—she can stop checking whether the panel is visible and just talk.

You control it under **Settings → Privacy & Stealth**. The honest framing: stealth is a concrete setting for a concrete risk, not a magic cloak that lets you skip preparation—you still own your answers, your delivery, and your honesty about experience, exactly as our earlier essay on [what is an interview copilot](/blog/what-is-interview-copilot) spells out.

## FAQ

**Do I need the desktop app to use Interview CoPilot™?** For live sessions, practice, reports, and screen help—yes. The desktop app is where the current experience runs; the website and the older desktop version behave differently and are not the primary path.

**What happened to the separate tools, like Mock Interview or Career Coach?** They were never really separate—they are now explicitly capabilities inside Interview CoPilot™. Practice covers the old mock and coaching ground, anchored to Goals and powered by debriefs.

**Is there a free trial?** No. The free plan includes practice; live Copilot sessions, Stealth Mode, and automatic debriefs require a paid plan. Download the app to see the workflow before you decide.

**What interview types does Practice support?** Eight: general, coding, system design, behavioral and leadership, product and case, data and ML, salary negotiation, and recruiter screen. You pick the type in Preflight when you launch practice from a Goal.

**What is Re-drill?** Targeted practice on the weak spots your previous debriefs identified—instead of spinning through generic questions, your next session starts where you stalled last time.

**How does Screen Help work for coding questions?** Auto-capture reads the screen when a technical question needs visual context; Solve screen triggers it manually; the Results panel shows full written solutions beside the answer feed and saves them into the debrief.
