**Today is our bet that the next leap in personal AI is not a smarter reply—it is an AI personal agent that stays with you.** One that remembers, notices, and follows through across the messy span of a real life. Below is why we think that matters and what we refuse to compromise on. I'm Junyuan, founder of Today—this is the vision post, not the spec sheet.

Personal software shaped my career—building tools where teams live every day taught me that **distribution and trust** matter as much as features. Today applies that lesson to AI: the agent has to be something people permit into calendar and mail, not a toy they open once and forget.

## 1. Why We're Building an AI Personal Agent

The most capable AI in history is already in everyone's pocket. It can plan, research, remember, write, and act. And yet, for almost everyone, it just sits there **waiting to be told what to do**. Power arrived before relationship. People have a genius on call and use it like a search box—not because they are wrong, but because the tools were built for answers, not for **presence**.

We think that is backwards. A capable **AI personal agent** should pay attention, remember what matters, notice change, and show up before you ask. Not a smarter chatbot—a companion that closes the gap between what AI *can* do and what your life actually gets from it. <a href="https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agents-vs-ai-assistants" rel="nofollow noopener">IBM's framing of assistants versus agents</a> puts it plainly: assistants react; agents pursue goals with tools and plans that can continue without you re-prompting every turn.

Three commitments anchor Today: **it knows you**, **it acts before you ask**, and **it walks with you** toward the life you want.

We are not trying to win a benchmark on a single prompt. We are trying to ship the kind of software you **leave running**—because it compounds context the way a great chief of staff compounds, quietly, over quarters. That ambition is why we call Today an **AI personal agent** in public, and why we wrote a separate definition post instead of hiding behind a vague "assistant" label. For the vocabulary—memory, initiative, confirm-before-execute—read [what an AI personal agent is](/blog/what-is-ai-personal-agent).

## 2. It Knows You — Memory You Can Trust

An agent that does not know you can only answer you; one that knows you can **help**. Most AI meets you as a stranger every time—the tab closes and the context vanishes. That is not an assistant or an agent. That is a vending machine.

![A hand lifting one card from a fan of handwritten index cards on a warm desk](/blog/images/meet-today/meet-today-knows-you.jpg)

Being known only works if you stay in control. Today holds memory you can read, edit, and erase—transparency as a design requirement, not a footnote. We did not want "personal context" to mean a black box the vendor owns and you hope is right. We wanted rows you can audit the way you audit a contact list: see the fact, fix the fact, delete the fact, move on.

In early Beta, people tell us the product "gets them." We treat that as the bar: not clever replies, but **continuity** that compounds. Trust is also **revocable**. Every connector you add should be removable without losing the core agent—conversation, memory, briefs. That design choice reflects how personal agents must behave: present by default, invasive never.

We are not building the loudest assistant in the feed. We are building the one you **keep**—because it remembers, prepares, and waits for your yes. Ecosystem assistants are racing toward the same problem—<a href="https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-introduces-siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant/" rel="nofollow noopener">Apple's Siri AI</a> promises personal context across messages, mail, and photos—but a dedicated personal agent still has to earn trust through **inspectable memory**, not only retrieval magic behind the glass.

If that resonates, try Today on Mac, iOS, or Android during Beta during the current early-access wave.

## 3. It Acts Before You Ask — Agency Without Spam

Knowing you is half the job. What you feel daily is what the agent **does** with that knowledge—quietly, before you ask. A good friend does not wait for a formal request before saying "don't forget tomorrow's thing." That is the proactivity we mean: timed to your day, not broadcast because the model can.

![A phone on a kitchen counter showing a single notification](/blog/images/meet-today/meet-today-proactive.jpg)

Chat is a terrible place to keep a life—everything scrolls away. A personal agent should **surface** what matters in views you return to, not bury it in threads you dig for. And it should work **while you live**: long-running tasks that stay alive across days instead of dying when a chat session ends.

We hold a strict notification bar—details in the product post—because an agent that pings everything gets muted, and then it helps no one. The comparison that matters here is not "more alerts versus fewer alerts" in the abstract; it is **signal tied to your day** versus **noise tied to model capability**. Classic assistants stay reactive by design; personal agents add initiative—and must pay for it with restraint.

Proactivity is not autonomy. Today will not send email, move calendar events, or message others without your explicit confirm. That line protects trust and keeps the product on the right side of "personal agent" versus "autonomous bot." If you want full autopilot, Today is the wrong tool today—and probably the wrong mental model for software this close to your identity. For how assistant-style reactive help compares to agent-style continuity, see our guide on [AI personal assistant vs AI personal agent](/blog/ai-personal-assistant-vs-ai-personal-agent).

## 4. It Walks With You — Life Arcs, Not Errands Only

The point was never to clear today's tasks slightly faster. It is to move your life forward across **months**—fitness, learning, savings, relationships, craft—not one errand at a time. Productivity software optimized the working slice of you. We disagree.

![A walker on a long ridge path through morning haze](/blog/images/meet-today/meet-today-long-thread.jpg)

Early Beta users use Today for work **and** for health routines, language practice, and long projects that do not fit a single prompt. That is the agent we want: one thread held across seasons. Lifestyle health workflows—meal planning, sleep rhythm, training—live on our [Healthcare hub](/healthcare) with clear **lifestyle support, not medical diagnosis** boundaries.

Getting in shape. Learning photography. Preparing for a baby. These are arcs, not checkboxes. An AI personal agent should remember where you are going and nudge you toward it—not reset every Monday.

Consider a founder preparing for a launch while traveling: the agent should connect **flight days**, **sleep debt**, and **promised deliverables** without a daily re-brief. Consider a parent returning to fitness after injury: memory should hold **clearance timing** and **prior load**, suggesting a lighter week before you overreach. That is a different job from delegating a deck or spreadsheet to a work sandbox—and the category split matters when buyers conflate them. Read [AI personal agent vs work agent](/blog/ai-personal-agent-vs-work-agent) when you need whole-day continuity separated from Cowork-style deliverable factories.

## 5. Capability Without the Hype

We do not lead with the machine, but warmth is not a substitute for follow-through. Under the surface, Today is a real agent stack: model orchestration, planning, memory retrieval, task queues, cross-device sync. We invest in internal evaluations so improvement is measurable—not merely claimed.

The unglamorous work matters: infrastructure that stays responsive, jobs that finish instead of stall, sync that lands on the right screen. **Knows you** and **acts before you ask** are demos until reliability makes them habits. Research on long-horizon AI systems keeps converging on the same insight: models are rarely the bottleneck anymore; **state, tooling, and evaluation** are. <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents" rel="nofollow noopener">Anthropic's writing on building effective agents</a> emphasizes disciplined scoping and human checkpoints—the same instincts we apply to calendar and mail.

We also believe **evaluation beats adjectives**. Internally, we test planning, memory retrieval, and connector success rates on scenarios drawn from real Beta days—not synthetic trivia questions. That discipline is how an AI personal agent earns the word **agent** instead of borrowing it for a landing page.

## 6. We'd Rather Be Honest Than Impressive

Today is not finished. An agent this close to your day earns trust in small things—the right time, the right fact, follow-through that does not drop—so we are shipping to people who want to grow with us and improving in the open. Features may change during Beta; the product is free for now ([Terms](/terms)). The goal was never more time in an app; it is more of your life back. When Today works, you think about the trip, the skill, the people—not the software.

Beta users shape the roadmap directly—feedback goes into memory, brief, and connector priorities on each release. That loop is intentional: personal agents should co-evolve with the people they serve, not ship as frozen demos.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why call it an AI personal agent instead of an assistant?

**Assistant** suggests answers on demand. **Agent** suggests persistence, initiative, and multi-step execution with your approval. Today does all three. We chose the label carefully so it means something precise—not marketing fog.

### Why start another AI company now?

Because capability without relationship wastes most of what models can do. The missing layer is not raw intelligence—it is an agent that **stays** with you across days and apps. That is the gap Today targets.

### What does "walks with you" mean in practice?

It means Today holds **long threads**—training blocks, learning goals, savings targets—not only today's calendar. Progress and setbacks feed memory so the next nudge fits where you actually are.

### Can I see and delete what Today remembers?

Yes. Transparency is core to trusting a personal agent. Memory is readable, editable, and deletable row by row—the product walkthrough in our main Today article covers the UI.

### Is Today only for work?

No. Work is one slice. Health, learning, relationships, and personal projects are first-class—see [Healthcare](/healthcare) for lifestyle examples with appropriate medical boundaries.
