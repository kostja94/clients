## TL;DR

- **Floatboat Flow Mode** is a voice-first writing layer inside Floatboat where speech, manual edits, and Agent collaboration happen in the **same document**—without stopping your train of thought.
- **Edit while you speak**: real-time **AI voice dictation** keeps flowing into the draft while you select a sentence and ask the Agent to refine it in place.
- **Batch annotations**: circle several spots in a long doc, describe each change by voice, and hand the whole bundle to the Agent in one pass.
- **Live meeting output**: while a conversation is still running, discussion lines up as notes and can become a checklist with owners—before anyone opens a separate **AI meeting notes** tool after the call.
- **Version history with diff**: every voice and Agent edit is tracked; compare versions and roll back safely when you experiment.

**Floatboat Flow Mode** is **AI voice dictation** built for document work: you stay in the draft, the Agent stays beside you, and the calendar-driven workspace around it still handles prep, deadlines, and follow-up.

## 1. Flow Mode is live: expression without waiting on tools

If you write for a living—as a solo founder drafting a launch post, a consultant shaping a client proposal, or an operator turning meeting talk into a plan—you already know the friction. **Speech-to-text** gets words onto the page faster than typing, but most tools treat dictation as a separate mode: stop talking, fix the paragraph, copy a chunk into chat, paste the rewrite back, repeat. **AI meeting notes** apps capture calls well, yet the deliverable often arrives *after* the meeting, in another tab, disconnected from the doc you were trying to finish.

**Floatboat Flow Mode** is our answer to that split. It is not a standalone dictation utility and not a post-meeting summarizer bolted onto the side. It is an expression layer inside [Floatboat](https://floatboat.ai/) where **voice input, editing, Agent rewrites, meeting capture, and version control** share one surface—the document you are actually trying to ship.

The goal is simple and hard at the same time: **keep your flow**. You talk; the draft grows. You touch the keyboard when you want precision. The Agent works on the span you select, not on a detached chat thread. When the meeting is still going, the outline of next steps can already be on the table.

## 2. The problem: tools interrupt the sentence, not the other way around

Most writing stacks assume you will **switch contexts** to get help. Dictation apps— including strong system-wide tools such as <a href="https://wisprflow.ai/" rel="nofollow noopener">Wispr Flow</a>—excel at turning speech into clean text **wherever your cursor lives**. That is genuinely useful for email, Slack, and quick fields. What they typically do not optimize for is **long-form co-authoring**: a draft that evolves over twenty minutes of speech, mid-stream edits, and several Agent passes on different paragraphs in one file.

Meeting AI has a parallel gap. Mature **AI note takers** like <a href="https://otter.ai/" rel="nofollow noopener">Otter</a> and Fireflies are excellent at transcription, search, and post-call summaries. They are built around the **recording lifecycle**, not around a working document that must be ready *during* the conversation. If your job is to leave the call with an action list stakeholders can react to—not a transcript to process later—the center of gravity is still wrong.

Chat-based writing assistants add a third interruption. Paste a section into Claude or ChatGPT, get a rewrite, paste back. The intelligence is real; the **document is not the runtime**. For solopreneurs who already juggle calendar prep, client calls, and shipping copy, that extra loop is where momentum dies.

We built Flow Mode around a different center: **the document is the workspace**, voice is the primary input, and the Agent is a collaborator inside the same file—aligned with Floatboat's broader **Calendar-Driven** story that work is triggered by what is on your schedule, not by whichever chat window you remembered to open. For the paradigm contrast in depth, see [Calendar-Driven AI vs Chat-Based AI](/blog/calendar-driven-ai-vs-chat-ai).

## 3. What Floatboat Flow Mode does (in plain terms)

Flow Mode ships as four capabilities that mirror how people actually write and meet. You can use them independently; together they cover the path from first spoken draft to safe revision.

### 3.1 Edit while you speak

Traditional **voice typing** often locks you into a record-then-fix rhythm: finish the take, then edit. Flow Mode keeps **real-time transcription** running into the document while you remain free to change words directly. When a sentence is close but not quite right, select it and hand that span to the Agent—without stopping the broader dictation session.

In practice that looks like a long thought landing in the doc as you talk, a quick manual tweak to a phrase, then a targeted Agent rewrite on the line that needs polish. The Agent panel reflects the shift from passive capture to active collaboration; the rest of the paragraph stays yours. If you have used selection-based voice editing in tools like Aqua Voice's Edit Mode, the interaction will feel familiar—but here it is native to a **persistent Agent workspace**, not a cross-app keyboard layer alone.

This is the core **AI voice dictation** difference we care about: dictation is not an endpoint; it is the continuous feed into a document you and the Agent co-own.

### 3.2 Batch annotations for long documents

Short prompts work until they do not. A brand story, a policy draft, or a multi-section proposal often needs **many small edits**—tighten tone on line four, swap a vague adjective on line twelve, add a number on line eighteen. Describing each fix in serial chat is tedious; marking up a PDF and hoping someone applies it is worse.

Flow Mode lets you **select a phrase**, describe the change by voice, and park that note in a bundle while recording continues. Voice notes land in a buffer first; you choose when to insert them so you never lose a half-formed instruction mid-sentence. When the bundle is ready—three locations, three intents—you send the package to the Agent once. Each chip keeps its **line anchor**, so rewrites land where you meant, not where the model guessed.

For anyone who edits long markdown or briefs inside Floatboat, this is the difference between "talk to chat about the doc" and "talk *to the doc* with structured intent."

### 3.3 Live meeting output—not just post-call notes

Flow Mode's meeting view records who said what as the conversation unfolds—speaker by speaker, line by line. That alone saves scramble time; the deeper move is **editing earlier lines while the meeting continues**. Someone revises an action wording; you update the live line without breaking capture.

When the discussion has moved forward enough, you push the latest context to the Agent. The output is not a wall of transcript—it is a **working plan**: a title, checkable items, and owners attached. The meeting can still be running when the draft plan appears. That complements Floatboat's existing [AI meeting preparation](/blog/ai-meeting-preparation) pipeline (context before the call) and [AI follow-up automation](/blog/ai-follow-up-automation) (structured next steps after)—Flow Mode fills the **during** gap that note-takers rarely optimize for.

We are not claiming to replace dedicated conferencing bots for every team; Otter and similar tools still win on ecosystem breadth and meeting search. Flow Mode is for operators who want **deliverables in the doc**, not only a searchable recording elsewhere.

### 3.4 Version history when you experiment boldly

Voice and Agent edits make drafts move fast—which is the point—and fast movement makes people hesitate unless rollback is obvious. Flow Mode records **version history** with human-readable labels and timestamps. Open an earlier revision, see **diff** against the current text, and revert in one action if an Agent pass overshot.

That safety net matters for solo founders who draft in public: you can try an aggressive rewrite on the opening paragraph, compare, and step back without archaeology in "undo" or orphaned chat threads.

## 5. A quick scenario: from spoken draft to signed-off plan

Picture a product launch week. Monday morning you open a launch memo and turn on Flow Mode. For fifteen minutes you talk through positioning—no outline required upfront—and the sections appear as you speak. You select the headline sentence and ask the Agent for a sharper hook; only that span changes.

Tuesday you run a brand review on a longer story. You highlight three awkward phrases, dictate three fixes into the annotation bundle without stopping the room's discussion recording, insert each note, and send the batch to the Agent once. Wednesday's client workshop is captured line by line; mid-call you revise one action item wording; before hang-up you generate a checklist with owners. Thursday you compare today's intro against Monday's version, keep the better hook, and revert a paragraph that drifted too casual.

At no point did you export the doc to a chat box or wait until Friday to "process the recording." That is the workflow Flow Mode is designed for.

## FAQ

### Is Floatboat Flow Mode the same as speech-to-text or a meeting bot?

Not exactly. **Speech-to-text** tools focus on converting audio to text—valuable, and Flow Mode includes real-time capture—but Flow Mode also supports in-document editing, Agent collaboration on selections, batched voice annotations, live meeting plans, and version diff. Dedicated **AI note takers** optimize the recording lifecycle; Flow Mode optimizes the **document** that must exist when the conversation ends.

### How is Flow Mode different from system-wide dictation apps like Wispr Flow?

Products like **Wispr Flow** are strong at polished dictation across any app on your machine—a different and legitimate job shape. **Floatboat Flow Mode** is document-centric inside the Floatboat workspace: Agent rewrites, annotation bundles, meeting-to-plan output, and calendar-linked Agent context in one place. Many users may still use system dictation elsewhere; Flow Mode is for drafts and plans that live in your Agent workspace.

### Can I edit while dictation is still running?

Yes—that is the namesake behavior. Flow Mode is built so **voice input does not force a full stop** before you touch the text or invoke the Agent on a selection. You choose when to pause talking; the doc remains editable throughout.

### Does Flow Mode replace Floatboat's calendar-driven meeting prep and follow-up?

No. Flow Mode adds a **during-meeting / during-draft** layer. [AI meeting preparation](/blog/ai-meeting-preparation) still handles pre-call context; [AI follow-up automation](/blog/ai-follow-up-automation) still structures what happens after. Flow Mode reduces the manual gap between speaking and a shippable artifact in the middle.

### What if an Agent edit goes wrong?

Use **version history**: open a prior revision, review the diff, and roll back to a known-good state. Flow Mode is designed so experimentation does not require copying files or losing chat-side alternatives.
