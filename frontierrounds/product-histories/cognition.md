# Cognition

> The autonomous coding-agent lab that went from Devin's launch to a US$48B valuation in 18 months

> 产品融资时间线档案 · 类型：product · 数据来自 frontierrounds.com Product Histories（`/products/cognition`）

## 概览

Cognition (Cognition AI) is the San Francisco-based startup behind Devin, the first fully autonomous AI software engineer — founded in late 2023 by competitive-programming champions Scott Wu (CEO), Steven Hao and Walden Yan, holders of ten IOI gold medals collectively. It launched Devin in March 2024 alongside a US$21M Founders Fund-led seed, then raised a ~US$175M Series A at ~US$2B within weeks. A ~US$4B Series B led by Lux Capital and 8VC followed (March 2025), then a US$400M+ Series C at US$10.2B led by Founders Fund (September 2025), a US$1B+ Series D at US$26B led by Lux/General Catalyst/8VC (May 2026), and — announced 8 September 2026 — a US$2B Series E at US$48B led by new investors Andreessen Horowitz and Accel. The valuation nearly doubled from US$26B in four months, on the back of run-rate revenue rising from US$492M (May) to ~US$900M (September). Along the way Cognition acquired the Windsurf IDE (July 2025, adding ~US$82M ARR) and counts Mercedes-Benz, NASA JPL, Goldman Sachs, Citi, the U.S. Army and Navy among its customers.

## 叙事章节

*The Story Behind the Numbers*

### From IOI Gold Medals to Devin's Launch
The three co-founders — competitive-programming champions with ten IOI gold medals between them — launched Devin in March 2024 as "the first AI software engineer," an agent that plans, codes, debugs and deploys end to end inside its own IDE, shell and browser. The launch post carried a US$21M Founders Fund-led round and a who's-who of angels (the Collisons, Elad Gil, Sarah Guo), and within weeks a ~US$175M Series A at ~US$2B made it one of the fastest unicorns in AI history.

### The Revenue Ramp That Defies AI-coding Skeptics
Devin's disclosed run-rate went from US$1M (September 2024) to US$73M (June 2025) — 73x in nine months. The July 2025 Windsurf acquisition added another ~US$82M ARR and an AI-native IDE; by the Series D (May 2026) company-wide run-rate hit US$492M, and by the Series E (September 2026) it neared US$900M. Cognition is reportedly targeting US$4–5B annualized revenue by end-2026, though it still operates at a loss with compute and server leasing estimated near US$800M a year.

### Windsurf, and the Parallel Google Deal
Cognition's July 2025 acquisition of the Windsurf IDE (formerly Codeium) was the deal that folded a rival coding tool's enterprise base into Devin — while, about a week earlier, Google executed a separate ~US$2.4B "reverse-acquihire" taking Windsurf's CEO and ~40 senior R&D staff to Google DeepMind. Cognition got the product, IP, ~US$82M ARR and ~200 employees; Google got the founding leadership. Two more acqui-hires followed in July 2026 (TierZero, then The Interaction Company's Poke).

### Enterprise Proof Points as the Moat
Cognition's named customers span the most demanding organizations: Mercedes-Benz (whose pilot cut a COBOL modernization from eight months to eight days), NASA JPL, Goldman Sachs, Citi, the U.S. Army and Navy, Dell, Palantir, Santander, Itaú. FedRAMP High In-Process status and a DOE Genesis Mission MOU underscore the federal/regulated wedge — the harder-to-replicate layer that distinguishes Devin from consumer coding tools.

### A Valuation Priced as Infrastructure
The Series E's US$48B against ~US$900M run-rate implies a ~53x multiple — the territory where investors price category dominance, not incremental growth. The round's lead is the subplot: a16z, a major Cursor backer that profited from its SpaceX sale, led a round in Cursor's direct competitor, signaling that AI coding is seen as far from winner-take-all and that autonomous agents have crossed from productivity tool to infrastructure layer.
