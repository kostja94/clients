## TL;DR

TikTok Shop's fees are no longer the bargain they were in 2024. In January 2026 the platform ended its promotional 1.8% commission; on August 4, 2026 it raised the standard referral fee for most US non-food categories from 6% to 8%, with beauty and personal care above that. The referral fee is only the headline: affiliate commissions you set, Fulfilled by TikTok logistics, and ad spend stack on top, and total platform-tied costs can land between roughly 15% and 35% of GMV for a typical order. This article maps every layer with sources, walks through both hikes, and gives you a margin model to test before you commit inventory.

- Referral fee: 8% standard on most US non-food categories as of August 2026 — up from 6% in January and a promotional 1.8% before that
- Affiliate commissions: typically 5%–25%, seller-set, and paid only on orders attributed to creators
- Fulfillment: US sellers must use TikTok logistics or approved providers since February 2026; FBT runs roughly $2.86–$4.28 per unit
- Blended cost: total platform-tied costs usually land between roughly 15% and 35% of GMV before ads — category and order value decide where
- Ads: co-funded Q4 ad-credit programs can offset paid traffic, but spend is a decision you control, not a fee

## Why "8% commission" is only the headline of the cost story

Ask "how much does it cost to sell on TikTok Shop" and most answers quote the referral commission — 8% these days — as if it were the whole story. It is not. The referral fee is one of four layers that stack on every order: the platform's referral commission, the affiliate commission you set for creators, fulfillment and shipping, and advertising or promotional spend. For buyer-side trust questions and promo-code stacking at checkout, see our [is TikTok Shop legit guide](/blog/is-tiktok-shop-legit) and [promo codes guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-promo-codes). Two of those layers are percentages, one is often a fixed dollar amount per unit, and one is entirely your choice, which is why the same "8% headline" produces very different real costs for a $15 impulse buy and a $60 home-goods order.

That difference is why this article exists: single-number summaries smooth over the variation that decides whether a shop is profitable. Everything here covers the US market only, is verified as of August 2026, and carries a source you can check. The model below is the framework to test your products against before you commit inventory.

The scale context matters: TikTok Shop is no longer a side experiment. Momentum Commerce estimates the platform is on pace to clear $32 billion in annualized US gross merchandise volume, per [Ecommerce Times' reporting on the US GMV milestone](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-gmv-hits-32b-annualized-as-algorithm-shifts-favor-inventory-heavy-sellers/), and global GMV hit an estimated $50.3 billion in the first half of 2026, per [Momentum Works data covered by 36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/3937372453715080). Growth and tightening economics have arrived together — exactly what the fee changes below show.

## The 2026 fee stack: every layer, with sources

The table below summarizes the four layers as of August 2026 for US sellers. Each number is a range, not a guarantee: rates differ by category, and your settlement statement is the final authority.

| Layer | What it is | Typical cost (as of August 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Referral commission | Platform fee on net GMV | 8% on most US non-food categories; beauty & personal care 8%+; some sub-categories reported higher |
| Affiliate commission | Seller-set payout to creators | Typically ~5%–25%, varies by category and program type |
| Fulfillment (FBT / TikTok Shipping) | Pick, pack, ship, plus storage | Roughly $2.86–$4.28 per unit; first 60 days of storage free |
| Payment processing | Reported as a separate line in some breakdowns | Roughly 1.8%–2% of each sale |
| Refund & return admin | Costs triggered by returns | Refund admin fee ~20% of the referral fee (capped around $5/SKU); return handling ~$3 per return |

Two caveats before the detail. Some fee breakdowns treat the referral fee as all-inclusive; others add a separate payment-processing line, which analysts put between roughly 1.8% and 2% of each sale, per [Ecommerce Times](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shop-in-2026-the-platform-that-rewired-social-commerce-2/) and [LiveShopFront](https://liveshopfront.com/tiktok-shop-seller-fees-2026-breakdown). Check your own Seller Center statement to see which model applies. Refunds also carry admin costs — sellers report a refund administration fee of about 20% of the referral fee (capped near $5 per SKU) plus return handling, per [Beancount's seller bookkeeping breakdown](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/25/tiktok-shop-seller-bookkeeping-fees-commissions-guide). On a high-return category, that hidden layer matters as much as the headline percentage.

### Layer 1: Referral commission — the fee that moved twice this year

The referral fee is the platform's cut of each sale, calculated on net GMV before sales tax and customer-paid shipping. As of August 4, 2026, the standard rate for most US non-food categories is 8%, up from 6%, according to [Ecommerce Times' reporting on the commission hike](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-commission-hike-is-reshaping-seller-economics-3/). Beauty and personal care sit at or above 8%, and some later reporting puts select beauty and electronics sub-categories between 9.5% and 11% ([Ecommerce Times' US merchant program audit](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-merchant-program-in-2026-a-full-audit/)). Food and grocery remain lower, around 5%. The takeaway: "8%" hides real category variation, and the direction is consistently upward.

### Layer 2: Affiliate commissions — the layer you control

This is the cost most fee guides understate, because it is not set by TikTok — it is set by you, in the affiliate marketplace, and only paid on orders that creators attribute. Rates typically range from roughly 5% to 25%, varying by category and program type, per [TikAdSuite's fee breakdown](https://tikadsuite.com/blog/tiktok-shop-fees/). A 15% beauty commission on a $40 sale is $6.00 to the creator. Because this layer is discretionary, margins are won or lost here — a shop leaning on affiliate traffic is renting discovery, while an organic-content shop avoids the layer entirely. For a creator-side view of the same commissions, our [TikTok Shop affiliate side-hustle guide](/blog/tiktok-affiliate-side-hustle) covers that perspective.

### Layer 3: Fulfillment and shipping — the fixed-dollar trap

TikTok proposed ending independent seller shipping in early 2026 — [36Kr reported](https://www.36kr.com/p/3937372453715080) the plan as forcing US sellers toward platform logistics or an approved provider list — but the mandate was [reversed in mid-February 2026](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/tiktok-halts-plan-to-end-independent-shipping-for-u-s-sellers-after-backlash/), with "Seller Shipping remains unchanged" per the reversal. The practical pressure on fulfillment still landed through the algorithm and through Fulfilled by TikTok's incentives rather than a hard ban. Fulfilled by TikTok (FBT) handles storage, picking, packing, and last-mile delivery at roughly $2.86–$4.28 per unit depending on weight and order size — $3.58 for a single-unit order, $2.86 per unit on multi-item orders, with storage free for the first 60 days, per [Eightx's take-rate analysis](https://eightx.co/blog/tiktok-shop-real-take-rate) and [TikAdSuite's fee breakdown](https://tikadsuite.com/blog/tiktok-shop-fees/). The structural point: this layer is a fixed dollar amount. The same $3.58 is about 9% of a $40 sale, 14% of a $25 sale, and 24% of a $15 one — cheap products structurally cannot win here.

The mandate also reshapes who benefits: TikTok's ranking system now weights estimated domestic delivery time and warehouse origin, so sellers with US-based inventory are gaining visibility while cross-border fulfillment is throttled, per [Ecommerce Times' analysis of the algorithm shift](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-gmv-hits-32b-annualized-as-algorithm-shifts-favor-inventory-heavy-sellers/). For the verification and fulfillment detail, see our [US domestic seller guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-domestic-seller); and because return-handling costs can scale with account health, the fulfillment layer connects directly to your [TikTok Shop performance score](/blog/tiktok-shop-performance-score).

### Layer 4: Advertising and promotions — the accelerator you choose

Ads are the one layer that is not a fee at all, but they belong in the stack because most scaled shops pay them. TikTok has shifted toward a unified Commerce Ads dashboard and pre-announced co-funded holiday programs for Q4 2026, such as Co-funded Ads and Smart Promotion (see [TikTok Shop Seller Center documentation](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university)). These programs subsidize spend rather than eliminate it, and eligibility varies. Treat ads as a variable cost allocated per order — total ad spend divided by attributed orders — and model it separately from the mandatory layers.

A quieter point belongs in this section: the content that generates organic traffic is itself a production cost. Roughly 40% of attributed US GMV still flows through video content (Shop tab accounts for about half, per [Momentum Works data via AMZ123](https://www.amz123.com/kx/jd4uLCso)), and producing shoppable videos at volume is a real line item. Sellers choose among freelancers, in-house editors, and AI tools — Moras, for US TikTok Shop video generation, sits alongside CapCut and manual workflows. Which fits depends on your SKU count and content budget, not on which tool is loudest.

## How two commission hikes changed the margin math in 2026

The single most important fact here is timing: TikTok Shop raised the standard commission twice in eight months, and sellers who priced against the old rate card are feeling the squeeze. The promotional period that subsidized early adoption ended in January 2026; the August increase compounded it.

| Period | Standard referral fee (US, non-food) | Cost on a $40 sale |
|---|---|---|
| Through 2025 (promotional window) | ~1.8% for eligible sellers | ~$0.72 |
| January 2026 | 6% standard; beauty & personal care 8% | ~$2.40–$3.20 |
| August 4, 2026 | 8% standard; beauty & personal care 8%+ | ~$3.20+ |

The January change is documented by [Ecommerce Times' review of the platform's seller economics](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shop-in-2026-the-platform-that-rewired-social-commerce-2/), which describes the shift from a promotional 1.8% to a standard 6% on most categories, with beauty and personal care climbing to 8%. The August change is documented separately by [Ecommerce Times' report on the US commission hike](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-commission-hike-is-reshaping-seller-economics-3/), which confirms the move from 6% to 8% on most non-food categories. Two sources, two hikes, one direction.

What does the combined effect look like in dollars? On a $40 sale, the referral line has gone from roughly $0.72 in the promotional era to about $3.20 today — a four-fold increase on that single line item, before any other layer. A seller moving 1,000 orders a month is therefore carrying roughly $2,500 more per month in referral fees than a seller who built pricing during the subsidized window. That is the kind of assumption a margin model needs to carry explicitly — the answer changes depending on which rate card you are still pricing against.

## What a $40 order actually nets you

Numbers matter more than percentages, so here is the model worked out for three realistic orders. The scenarios are illustrative fee-model calculations, not profit guarantees — actual net depends on your category rate, affiliate plans, return rate, and COGS. Every number is an assumption you can change.

| Scenario | Assumptions | Platform-tied costs | Net before ads & COGS |
|---|---|---|---|
| $40 beauty, affiliate-driven | 8% referral, 15% affiliate, FBT $3.58, ~2% payment | $13.58 (~34% of GMV) | $26.42 |
| $25 gadget accessory, organic | 8% referral, no affiliate, FBT $3.58, ~2% payment | $6.08 (~24% of GMV) | $18.92 |
| $15 impulse item, affiliate-driven | 8% referral, 15% affiliate, FBT $3.58, ~2% payment | $7.33 (~49% of GMV) | $7.67 |

The contrast between the first and second scenarios is the core lesson of the stack. The $40 beauty order pays a 15% affiliate commission because it is attributed to a creator; the $25 gadget order carries no commission because it came organically. The affiliate layer is worth $6.00 on the first order and $0 on the second — which is why "what does TikTok Shop take?" has no single answer. After COGS the gap widens further: the $40 beauty item with 70% gross margin nets about $14.42 before ads, versus roughly $0.17 for the $15 impulse item on an affiliate-attributed order.

That last line is the quiet killer: fixed-dollar FBT plus two percentage layers can consume nearly half of a low-ticket sale before product cost is subtracted, as [Eightx's take-rate analysis](https://eightx.co/blog/tiktok-shop-real-take-rate) shows in similar terms. Low-price products are not impossible on TikTok Shop, but they require multi-unit orders (which lower the per-unit FBT rate) or gross margins most accessories cannot deliver.

## When the fee stack kills your margin — and when it doesn't

AOV does more to decide your fee burden than any other variable you control, because two layers scale with price while one is fixed. The table below derives fee shares from the same assumptions as the scenarios above (8% referral, ~2% payment, $3.58 FBT, affiliate rates of 10%–15% depending on category); it is a planning tool, not a rate card.

| Order value | Fee share, organic | Fee share, affiliate-attributed | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| $15 | ~34% | ~49% | FBT alone is ~24%; margins require exceptional unit economics |
| $25 | ~24% | ~34% | Viable with strong gross margin; every dollar of AOV helps |
| $40 | ~19% | ~34% | Mid-range sweet spot for most categories |
| $60 | ~16% | ~28% | Affiliate layer becomes the biggest variable |
| $100 | ~14% | ~24% | Premium pricing absorbs fixed fulfillment cleanly |

The pattern is consistent: fee share compresses as order value rises, and the affiliate mix decides which end of each range you land on. For a $60 home-goods item, platform-tied costs of roughly 16%–28% of GMV sit next to a gross margin kitchen categories can usually support. For a $15 impulse gadget, the same math can wipe out a 50% margin before product is counted. Category economics gate the whole model — beauty's high gross margins absorb an 8%+ referral fee; thin-margin electronics do not — which is why category reporting (beauty and electronics cited between 9.5% and 11% in some sub-categories, per [Ecommerce Times' merchant program audit](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-merchant-program-in-2026-a-full-audit/)) matters more than the headline 8%.

Two practical rules follow. First, price for the top of the range: if your blended cost can land at 30% of GMV, model 30%, not 19%. Second, treat multi-unit orders as a fee-management lever — bundling and quantity deals convert fixed FBT dollars into a smaller share of a larger order, which is the same insight behind [Cruva's margin guidance](https://cruva.com/blog/tiktok-shop-fees/). The platform's own direction supports this: sellers with US-based inventory and faster delivery signals are gaining visibility under the current algorithm, making domestic stock a fee-adjacent advantage.

## TikTok Shop vs Amazon vs eBay: the comparison sellers actually need

Headline percentages are the wrong way to compare marketplaces, because each platform structures its take differently. The table below lines up the components, using official fee documentation for Amazon and eBay alongside the August 2026 TikTok Shop figures from this article.

| Component | TikTok Shop (US, as of Aug 2026) | Amazon (US) | eBay (US) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core commission | 8% most non-food categories; beauty 8%+ | ~8%–17% by category; most ~15%, payment included | Final value fee ~12.7%–15.3%; 13.6% for most categories (12.7% with a Store), payment included |
| Payment processing | Reported as a separate ~1.8%–2% line in some breakdowns | Included in referral fee | Included in final value fee |
| Fulfillment | TikTok logistics required for US sellers; FBT ~$2.86–$4.28/unit | FBA optional: ~$3.22+/unit plus storage | Self-fulfill or your own 3PL |
| Monthly fee | None | $39.99/month (Professional plan, optional) | None required (free Starter plan) |
| Affiliate/creator layer | Seller-set commissions, typically 5%–25%, on attributed orders | Creator Connections and Associates (lower typical rates) | No native creator layer |

The fair reading: TikTok Shop's headline 8% looks cheaper than Amazon's typical 15% referral fee ([Amazon's published referral fee table](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing)) and eBay's typical 13.6% final value fee (12.7% with a Basic Store, per [eBay's fee schedule for sellers](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-sellers?id=4822)) — and for organic orders on higher-ticket items, it often is. The comparison flips once the other layers appear: TikTok Shop pairs its referral fee with mandatory platform fulfillment and, on any creator-attributed order, a seller-set affiliate commission. A blended platform-tied cost of 15%–35% of GMV on TikTok Shop is roughly the same neighborhood as Amazon FBA's all-in costs and above eBay's leaner fee-only model.

None of this makes one platform "better." Amazon brings unmatched search volume and category breadth at a higher baseline fee; eBay offers a lower fee-only structure with less discovery momentum; TikTok Shop offers the fastest discovery flywheel in exchange for a tighter cost stack and a content obligation. Model the full stack for your specific SKU on each platform — commission, fulfillment, and traffic-acquisition cost — rather than comparing a single percentage.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does TikTok Shop charge sellers in 2026?

For US sellers, the standard referral fee is 8% on most non-food categories as of August 2026, with beauty and personal care at 8% or above. On top of that come affiliate commissions you set (typically 5%–25%), platform fulfillment at roughly $2.86–$4.28 per unit, and payment processing around 1.8%–2%. A typical blended cost lands between roughly 15% and 35% of GMV before ads.

### Is there a monthly fee to sell on TikTok Shop?

No. TikTok Shop does not charge a monthly selling subscription the way Amazon's Professional plan does. Costs are transaction-based: you pay referral fees, fulfillment costs, and any affiliate commissions on orders you actually sell, plus optional advertising spend. There is no flat monthly fee just for keeping the shop open.

### What percentage of each sale does TikTok Shop take?

There is no single percentage — the mix depends on your category, order value, and whether the order was attributed to a creator. On a $40 beauty order with a 15% affiliate commission, platform-tied costs run about 34% of GMV; on the same order sold organically, roughly 19%. The referral fee alone is 8% on most non-food categories.

### Do TikTok Shop fees include affiliate commissions?

No. Affiliate commissions are separate from TikTok's referral fee and are set by you in the affiliate marketplace. They are paid only on orders attributed to creators, typically ranging from 5% to 25% depending on category and program. Because this layer is discretionary, it is the biggest variable you control.

### Are TikTok Shop fees higher than Amazon's?

TikTok Shop's 8% referral fee is lower than Amazon's typical 15%, but Amazon's fee includes payment processing, TikTok Shop can add a separate payment line, and TikTok adds affiliate commissions and mandatory fulfillment. Amazon FBA's all-in costs and TikTok Shop's full stack land in a similar range, so comparing one percentage without the other layers is misleading.

### Can US sellers still fulfill orders themselves in 2026?

Since February 25, 2026, US sellers must use TikTok's logistics services or an approved provider list; independent self-fulfillment with your own carrier labels is no longer available. Fulfilled by TikTok (FBT) covers storage, picking, packing, and delivery at roughly $2.86–$4.28 per unit, with the first 60 days of storage free.

### What happens to the referral fee when a buyer returns an item?

Returns trigger their own costs. Sellers report a refund administration fee of roughly 20% of the referral fee (capped near $5 per SKU) plus return-handling charges of about $3 per return on FBT orders. Return-related cost shares can also scale with your Shop Performance Score, so high-return categories carry a hidden fee layer on top of the four main ones.
