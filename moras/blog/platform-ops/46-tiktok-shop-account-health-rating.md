## The Account Health Rating is TikTok Shop's new enforcement system

TikTok Shop retired its Violation Points framework and replaced it with the Account Health Rating (AHR) in July 2026, after publishing the full requirements document in the US Academy Policy Center on June 25, 2026. The replacement was previewed months in advance: the platform announced the July switch in May, and a score preview appeared in Seller Center the same month, giving attentive sellers a head start on everyone else, as [PPC Land's timeline of the rollout](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-blocks-new-listings-and-campaigns-when-seller-ahr-hits-150/) documents.

The AHR is a color-coded number from 0 to 1,000 that measures how compliant your shop has been over the last 180 days, according to TikTok's [Account Health Rating Requirements](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6750828276418350&lang=en) in the US Academy Policy Center. It is not a report card for buyer experience — it is an enforcement system. A violation deducts points, and when the score crosses certain thresholds, TikTok restricts what you can do: creating new listings, enrolling in campaigns, and ultimately keeping the account open. You can see your live score and any pending enforcement actions in Seller Center, where the AHR has been visible since the preview launched in May 2026.

The shift in logic matters more than the change in numbers. Under Violation Points, a seller was either under the line and fine, or over it and punished. Under the AHR, the score compounds continuously — a cluster of moderate infractions can sink an account even when no single violation is catastrophic, a point [TechTimes made](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320624/20260715/tiktok-shop-bans-ai-voices-live-commerce-streams-violations-now-dent-account-health-score.htm) in its coverage of the enforcement change. That is a different operating model, and the sellers most exposed are the ones who built their shops around occasional violations and a quarterly glance at the dashboard.

There is also a market context that makes this worth learning now. Related search interest in TikTok Shop seller tools has cooled through 2026, a leading indicator that the US seller pool is contracting as weaker accounts exit rather than adapt. For the sellers who stay, that means less competition — but only if the account stays in good standing. Understanding the AHR is no longer background knowledge; it is survival infrastructure for a TikTok Shop in 2026.

## How the 0–1,000 score works

Every US seller starts at 200 points when they join TikTok Shop. From there, the score moves in both directions: points are added for successfully fulfilling orders and for passing policy quizzes, and deducted when you breach a policy or miss a performance standard. Each point you earn or lose stays on your rating for 180 days, so the number you see today reflects roughly half a year of behavior — not last week, and not your lifetime record. TikTok does not publish a uniform point value for every action; reports describe earning roughly 4 points per 200 completed orders with a cap near 20 points per week, so recovery is steady rather than instant ([SellerOps' enforcement walkthrough](https://sellerops.io/tiktok-shop-us-account-health-rating-ahr-how-enforcement-really-works/), as of July 2026).

The score is color-zoned so you can read your standing at a glance.

| Zone | Score range | What it means |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Green | 200+ | Healthy account; standard operations |
| Orange | 51–199 | At risk of receiving milestone enforcement |
| Red | 50 or fewer | At risk of deactivation |

TikTok's guidance is to keep the AHR above 150 to avoid milestone restrictions, but note that the green zone starts at 200. A seller at 160 is not technically green — they are orange and one bad week away from the first enforcement tier. Treat 200 as the target and 150 as the hard floor, not the other way around. Because the window is 180 days, the score also rewards patience: points you earn now slowly push out older deductions, which means a disciplined month pays off in the number for half a year.

## The four milestone thresholds — and what each one costs you

When your score crosses 150, 100, 50, or 0, TikTok applies what it calls milestone enforcement actions on top of whatever restrictions already exist. The core penalties are consistent across the tiers and scale with severity.

| Threshold | New listings | Mega campaign enrollment | Additional effects |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| 150 | Blocked for 7 days | Blocked for 7 days | Traffic throttling begins |
| 100 | Blocked for 14 days | Blocked for 14 days | Reduced livestream traffic; removal from Shop Tab recommendations |
| 50 | Blocked for 28 days | Blocked for 28 days | Red zone; deactivation risk |
| 0 | — | — | Potential permanent account deactivation |

Mega campaigns are TikTok's large-scale promotional events, and losing access to them — plus the ability to list new products — is what makes the 150 tier hurt even for sellers with a healthy existing catalog. You can keep selling existing products, but you cannot grow, and in live-dependent categories the reduced livestream traffic at 100 can compound the damage quickly. At 0 points, TikTok may permanently deactivate the account, and accounts that repeatedly cross severe thresholds face heavier consequences ([AHR Requirements](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6750828276418350&lang=en), as of July 2026).

The penalties are cumulative, not per-drop. Dropping from 150 to 100 extends the enforcement window from 7 to 14 days; it does not restart a fresh 7-day clock. This is why a seller who catches the score at 155 and does nothing can be in a much worse position three weeks later than one who treats 150 as an emergency. One more consequence worth naming: traffic throttling and reduced recommendations act on a delay, so a seller can be losing visibility for days before the number in Seller Center catches up with what the algorithm is already doing.

## What the AHR replaced: Violation Points, on a different model

The AHR replaces a system that worked in a fundamentally different way, and most of the confusion around the new score comes from carrying over old assumptions. The old Violation Points model was a strike system: points accumulated when you violated a policy, they reset after 90 days, and a fixed ceiling meant permanent deactivation ([Parasocial Magazine's comparison](https://www.parasocialmagazine.com/news/tiktok-shop-swaps-strikes-for-account-health-rating/), as of July 2026).

| Dimension | Old system: Violation Points | New system: Account Health Rating |
|-----------|------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Scale | Points accumulate toward a fixed threshold | 0–1,000 score; sellers start at 200 |
| Reset cycle | Deductions reset after 90 days | 180-day rolling window; points decay as they age |
| Penalty trigger | Crossing a single line (48 points = deactivation, per reported coverage of the old non-US model) | Graduated milestones at 150/100/50/0 |
| Recovery | Wait for the reset | Earn points back through orders and quizzes |
| Logic | Pass/fail | Continuous and compounding |

The practical difference is that the old system forgave: a violation was a countable strike that aged out, and a seller could effectively wait out a bad period. The AHR does not forgive the same way — deductions linger for 180 days and interact with everything else you do. But it also rewards recovery, because earning points actively pushes the number back up rather than waiting on a timer. Sellers who learned to "serve the 90 days" under the old rules need to replace that reflex with a continuous score-management habit.

## What earns points and what costs points

The AHR is not a mystery box — its inputs are documented, and almost all of them are behaviors you already control in your daily operations. The table below separates the point movers into what restores your score and what drains it.

| Direction | Action | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Gain** | Fulfill eligible, completed orders | Reports describe ~4 points per 200 orders, capped near 20/week |
| **Gain** | Pass policy quizzes, including milestone quizzes | Can reduce the duration of milestone enforcement |
| **Gain** | Win an appeal or correct a violation | Deducted points can be restored earlier than the 180-day window |
| **Lose** | Breach a policy | Deductions scale with violation severity and frequency |
| **Lose** | Miss performance standards | E.g., late dispatch, seller-fault cancellations, slow after-sales |
| **Lose** | Violate content quality rules | AI voices or pre-recorded audio on livestreams count against the account |

The ordering principle is that severity and frequency both matter. A single severe violation can cost more than several minor ones, but repeated minor violations accumulate into the same danger zone — which is exactly how accounts without one catastrophic incident still end up at 150. The content quality dimension is new and easy to miss: rules published on May 23, 2026 require real-time verbal or sign-language communication on promotional livestreams, so sellers who automated live streams with AI-generated voices are now losing points on a daily basis ([PPC Land's summary of the quality rules](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shops-quality-rules-ban-ai-voices-and-still-images-from-lives/), as of July 2026).

## The policy changes that shipped with the AHR

The AHR rollout was bundled with two changes to the Store Rating metrics, and ignoring them means optimizing for the wrong numbers. First, the Customer Complaint Rate metric has been retired and replaced by After-Sales Handling Time (AHT) — a 60-day rolling average of how quickly you resolve after-sales requests. Second, negative review rate and seller-fault return/refund rate are no longer measured against fixed thresholds; they are now benchmarked against other sellers in your category, a shift [Canopy Management analyzed](https://canopymanagement.com/tiktok-shop-account-health-rating-changes/) in detail.

The AHT swap rewards a different behavior than the metric it replaces. Complaint rate measured how often buyers complained; AHT measures how fast you fix the problem once they do. A shop that batches after-sales tickets into an end-of-day block drags its 60-day average up every day it keeps that habit, even when complaint volume is low. The operational fix is boring but effective: templated responses for common return reasons, a same-day check of the after-sales inbox instead of an evening sweep, and a clear rule for who touches a ticket within the first few hours. If tickets regularly escalate, our [TikTok Shop customer service guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-customer-service) maps the escalation paths in Seller Center.

The category comparison is subtler and, in crowded niches, the more dangerous change. A shop can hold a 2% negative review rate for a year and suddenly read as below-median the moment peer comparison goes live, because the yardstick moved even though nothing about the shop did. The same logic applies to return rates. That means checking your category's benchmark matters as much as checking your own historical trend line — a flat personal trend can still mean falling behind.

The livestream rules deserve their own paragraph because they bind the new score to content, not just fulfillment. Since May 23, 2026, all verbal communication in promotional livestreams and shoppable videos must occur in real time, spoken or signed — AI-generated voices, pre-recorded narration, and radio-style audio are prohibited, and violations feed directly into account health. For sellers who built 24-hour "storefronts" on synthetic hosts, the economics changed overnight. If you produce recorded shoppable content, our [TikTok AI content rules explainer](/blog/tiktok-ai-content-rules) covers what is allowed and what must be labeled.

## AHR vs SPS: two systems, not one

Sellers routinely mix up the Account Health Rating with the Shop Performance Score, and the two are frequently discussed in the same breath — but they measure different things and are enforced differently. Treating them as one system leads to fixing the wrong number.

| System | Scale | What it measures | What it gates |
|--------|-------|------------------|---------------|
| Shop Performance Score (SPS) | 0–5 | Buyer-facing results: product quality, fulfillment, service | Affiliate access, campaigns, settlement speed, visibility |
| Account Health Rating (AHR) | 0–1,000 | Policy compliance and violations over 180 days | New listings, mega campaigns, account survival |

The SPS is the results system: it tracks how well your buyers experience your shop across six metrics like on-time delivery and negative review rate. The AHR is the compliance system: it tracks whether your shop is following the rules. They interact — the same late dispatch can dent both — but a shop can hold a perfect SPS and still lose the account on AHR, or run clean compliance while buyer-experience metrics throttle distribution. For the full breakdown of the six SPS metrics and the 3.5 threshold that controls affiliate access, see our [TikTok Shop Performance Score guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-performance-score).

In practice, you should watch both numbers with the same cadence and treat a dip in either as an operations problem, not a dashboard quirk. Because the AHR is compliance-focused and the SPS is results-focused, the fixes rarely overlap: a slow after-sales team is an SPS and an AHT problem, while an AI-voice violation is purely an AHR problem. The boundary is also why this article exists separately — most of what you read about "account health" conflates the two, and sellers who act on the wrong one pay for it twice.

## A monthly AHR maintenance workflow

The sellers who survive the AHR era will be the ones who treat the score as an operating metric rather than a report card. The workflow below is built around three questions: how often to check, what warning lines to respect, and how to recover when a drop happens anyway.

**Check twice a month during the first 90 days of the rollout, monthly after that.** The system is new, the enforcement copy changes as TikTok calibrates it, and category benchmarks are still settling. A twice-monthly check lets you catch a downward drift while the 180-day window still has room to absorb a recovery. Set the calendar reminder next to your settlement and ad-spend review so it does not get skipped.

**Respect two warning lines.** The hard floor is 150 — crossing it starts the enforcement clock. The soft warning line is 180, which gives you a buffer before violations start compounding into listing blocks. If your score prints below 180, treat it as a freeze on expansion decisions until it recovers above 200. And remember that the green zone starts at 200, not 151, so "above 150" is the floor, not the goal.

**Act on the inputs, not the number.** The score lags the behavior, so the monthly check should look at the metrics that feed deductions: late dispatch, seller-fault cancellations, after-sales handling time, and any open violations or appeals. A slow [shipping delay](/blog/tiktok-shop-shipping-delay) problem shows up in the score weeks after it starts — catching it at the metric level stops the damage early. If you are in a live-heavy category, add the livestream content rules to the same checklist, because an AI-voice violation is a repeatable daily deduction until you fix the stream.

**Have a recovery sequence ready before you need it.** If the score drops into enforcement territory: first, pass the milestone quiz, which can shorten the enforcement window. Second, file appeals on any deductions you believe are incorrect — the documentation requirements and appeal workflow are in our [TikTok Shop violation appeal guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-violation-appeal). Third, prioritize order fulfillment and after-sales speed to earn points back — every completed order moves the number up, and the 180-day window means older deductions age out on their own. None of this requires waiting out a reset the way the old system did; it requires active operation.

The final piece is account security, because an AHR cannot be maintained if the account is not yours anymore. Enable [two-step verification](/blog/tiktok-two-step-verification) on the TikTok login tied to Seller Center and review active sessions as part of the same monthly routine — account takeover stops operations regardless of what the score says.

## Conclusion

The Account Health Rating replaces the old pass/fail world of Violation Points with a continuous, compounding score — and it is the difference between a shop that can grow and a shop that is slowly being boxed in. Every US seller starts at 200 points on a 0–1,000 scale over a rolling 180 days. Crossing 150, 100, or 50 blocks new listings and mega campaigns for 7, 14, or 28 days, and hitting 0 risks permanent deactivation.

The good news is that the score is readable and mostly within your control. Points come back through fulfilled orders, policy quizzes, and successful appeals; deductions scale with how severe and how frequent the violations are. The 2026 policy bundle made it harder in three specific ways — after-sales speed now counts as a named metric, review and return rates are benchmarked against your category, and AI-voice livestreams are no longer acceptable — but every one of those changes is a behavior you can adjust.

Run the monthly check, respect the 180 warning line, keep the recovery sequence ready, and treat the AHR and the SPS as two numbers to watch rather than one confusing blob. The sellers who do that are the ones who will still be listing in a year — and in a thinning market, that is the entire game.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the TikTok Shop Account Health Rating?

The Account Health Rating (AHR) is a 0–1,000 score that measures your shop's policy compliance over the last 180 days. It replaced the Violation Points system in July 2026. Every seller starts at 200 points, gains points for fulfilled orders and policy quizzes, and loses points for violations and performance failures.

### What happens when my AHR drops to 150?

At 150 points, you lose the ability to create new listings and enroll in new mega campaigns for 7 days. At 100 points the same restrictions extend to 14 days with reduced livestream traffic, and at 50 points they extend to 28 days. Reaching 0 risks permanent account deactivation.

### How do I increase my TikTok Shop Account Health Rating?

Fulfill eligible orders (reported at roughly 4 points per 200 completed orders, capped near 20 per week), pass policy quizzes, and win appeals on incorrect deductions. Each point stays on your rating for 180 days, so older deductions age out on their own while earned points push the number up.

### Does the AHR replace the old Violation Points system?

Yes. The AHR replaced Violation Points in July 2026. Under the old system, deductions reset after 90 days and a fixed threshold meant permanent deactivation. The AHR uses a 180-day rolling window with graduated milestones and rewards active recovery instead of waiting out a reset.

### Is the Account Health Rating the same as the Shop Performance Score?

No. The SPS is a 0–5 buyer-experience score covering six metrics and gating affiliate access and settlement speed. The AHR is a 0–1,000 compliance score gating new listings and campaign access. They interact, but they are separate systems — fix the number that is actually wrong.
