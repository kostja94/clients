## TL;DR

A TikTok Shop violation is not the same as a low Account Health score — it is an enforcement ticket with its own deadlines, evidence rules, and recovery paths. US sellers get up to two appeals per violation (30 days for the first, 15 days after a rejection for the second); affiliate creators get one appeal within 30 days, and milestone enforcement actions cannot be appealed at all. The fastest win is often Correction before enforcement kicks in, not a formal dispute after the fact. This article walks through the Seller Center and Creator Studio appeal workflow, an evidence checklist by violation type, and how to recover from commission freezes and product promotion restrictions — so you can contest the right ticket with the right proof before the window closes.

- Sellers: Seller Center → Account Health → Violation Records → Correct, Appeal, or Quiz depending on ticket status
- Creators: violation ticket in Creator Health Record → one appeal within 30 days; commission freezes forfeit if you miss the window
- Risky-behavior violations (fraud, deception, repeat strikes) face stricter evidence and may permanently withhold commissions
- Content violations (misleading claims, unoriginal video, disclosure gaps) are fixable with policy-aligned proof and rectification plans
- After both seller appeals fail, escalation goes through your account manager or the TTS US Seller Growth Community — not a random support email

## Violation tickets are not the same as your health score

Most sellers and creators who panic after a TikTok Shop notice are reacting to the wrong number. Your [Account Health Rating (AHR)](/blog/tiktok-shop-account-health-rating) or Creator Health Rating (CHR) is a rolling compliance score — it tells you how close you are to milestone restrictions over 180 days. A violation ticket is a discrete enforcement event with its own penalty, timer, and appeal path. You can hold a decent health score and still receive a violation that freezes commissions overnight, or you can win an appeal that restores AHR points immediately without waiting for the 180-day window to age out deductions.

That distinction matters because the fix is different. Score management is continuous — fulfill orders, pass quizzes, keep after-sales response times tight. Violation recovery is episodic — read the ticket, pick the correct option before the clock expires, submit original evidence, and track the result in your inbox. TikTok notifies you by email and Seller Center or Creator inbox; for serious cases, additional channels may apply, per the [Violation & Appeal Guide](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6110391400302350&lang=en) (as of May 2026). If your AHR is within 10 points of a milestone threshold, you get a separate warning — but that warning is about score tiers, not a substitute for reading the violation record attached to the enforcement.

For affiliates who promote products but do not operate a shop, the creator-side ticket is what blocks commission payouts and product tagging — not the seller AHR dashboard. Treat every notification as a standalone case file, not a vague "account health" problem you can ignore until next month.

## Seller vs creator: two appeal systems on one platform

TikTok Shop runs parallel enforcement stacks for sellers and creators, and conflating them is the most common reason appeals fail before they are even read. Sellers manage violations in Seller Center under Account Health → Violation Records. Creators review tickets on the Creator Health Rating page and respond through the violation ticket linked in TikTok Studio or the creator monetization inbox.

The rules differ in ways that matter on day one.

| Dimension | US sellers | US affiliate creators |
|-----------|------------|----------------------|
| Where to find tickets | Seller Center → Account Health → Violation Records | Creator Health Record / violation ticket in Studio |
| Appeals allowed | Up to **2** per violation | **1** per violation |
| First appeal window | 30 calendar days from notification | 30 calendar days from enforcement date |
| Second appeal window | 15 calendar days after first rejection | Not available |
| Pre-enforcement option | **Correct** listing issues before penalty applies | Limited; depends on violation type |
| Milestone enforcement | AHR milestones at 150/100/50/0 — separate from ticket appeals | **Not appealable**, per [Creator Enforcement Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837869503317761&lang=en) |
| Commission impact | Fund holds, settlement delays, balance deductions | Commission freeze; may forfeit if appeal denied |
| Policy source | [Seller Enforcement Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=2380042836166443&lang=en) | [Creator Enforcement Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837869503317761&lang=en) |

Sellers also have a **Correct** path that creators rarely get: fix the listing, upload proof, and cancel enforcement before it applies. Creators more often land in post-enforcement appeal territory — especially when a video is flagged for misleading claims, missing disclosure, or unoriginal content. If you are both a shop owner and a creator account tied to that shop, check both surfaces; deactivation or an Order Volume Limit (OVL) of zero on the seller side can block e-commerce permissions on linked creator accounts simultaneously, per TikTok's connected-account rules in the Violation & Appeal Guide.

When you need human routing — for example, a ticket stuck in review past five business days — use the role-appropriate support channel from our [TikTok Shop customer service guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-customer-service): Seller Center chat or tickets for shops, TikTok Shop Studio Help for creators. Include the violation ID in the first message.

## Step-by-step: appealing a violation as a seller

The seller appeal path is documented in TikTok's [Violation & Appeal Guide](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6110391400302350&lang=en) and [Seller Enforcement Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=2380042836166443&lang=en) (as of August 2026). Follow these steps in order — skipping straight to Appeal when Correction is still available wastes your fastest option.

**Step 1 — Open the ticket immediately.** Log in to Seller Center → Account Health → Shop Health → Violation Records. Click the notification from your inbox or email. Each ticket shows Enforcement Actions (what penalty applies), Violation Reason, Violation Details (product or order IDs when relevant), and Next Steps with time-limited options.

**Step 2 — Read the status and choose your path.**

| Ticket status | What it means | Your move |
|---------------|---------------|-----------|
| Pending | Enforcement has **not** taken effect | **Correct** the listing, or **Appeal** within 12–72 hours on urgent cases |
| Enforcement action taken | Penalty is active | **Appeal** only |
| Under Review | Correction or appeal in progress | Wait; do not open duplicate tickets |
| Enforcement action cancelled | You won | Archive evidence; fix root cause |

**Step 3 — Correct (if available).** For listing-quality violations, update the live listing first, then upload proof (JPEG/PNG/PDF under 5 MB). AI Correction may offer a one-click fix — accepting it can auto-cancel the violation. Submitting without actually changing the listing returns an error. Correction means you acknowledge the listing had an issue; Appeal means you dispute the decision entirely.

**Step 4 — Appeal (if Correction failed or was not offered).** Go to View Details → Appeal. File within **30 calendar days** of notification. For violations where enforcement is not yet immediate, the appeal window may be **12–72 hours** — if approved, enforcement never applies. Upload your evidence package in one submission; incomplete files are auto-rejected.

**Step 5 — Track the decision.** Monitor Seller Center Inbox. If approved, enforcement is withdrawn and AHR points restore immediately — you do not wait 180 days. If rejected, you have **15 calendar days** to file a **second and final appeal** with **new evidence** not included in the first attempt.

**Step 6 — Escalate only after both appeals fail.** Unmanaged sellers may request additional guidance through the TTS US Seller Growth Community linked from the Violation & Appeal Guide. Escalation requires screenshots plus a completed appeal letter using TikTok's template — screenshots alone are not accepted. Assigned account managers can route cases differently; everyone else uses the community path.

Throughout the process, TikTok reviews at its sole discretion. Submitting an appeal does not guarantee approval — but missing a deadline guarantees you cannot contest that violation again.

## Step-by-step: appealing as an affiliate creator

Creator enforcement hits affiliates where it hurts: commission freezes, shoppable video limits, product tagging blocks, and — in repeated or severe cases — permanent withholding of earnings. The [Creator Enforcement Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837869503317761&lang=en) (updated July 31, 2026) defines the US rules.

**Step 1 — Locate the violation.** Open your Creator Health Record and the Violations section referenced in your TikTok monetization inbox. Note the enforcement date — the 30-day appeal clock starts there, not when you first noticed a smaller payout.

**Step 2 — Confirm the violation category.** Milestone enforcement actions — pre-set consequences for reaching violation point thresholds — **cannot be appealed**. Neither can some severe enforcement outcomes tied to repeated identical violations: creators who commit the **same policy violation six times within 90 days** may have e-commerce permissions removed and commissions frozen **regardless of CHR points**. If your ticket is a milestone action or falls under that repeat-strike rule, focus on prevention and support routing rather than a standard appeal form.

**Step 3 — Submit one appeal through the violation ticket.** Creators get **only one appeal per violation**, due within **30 calendar days** of enforcement. State clearly why the enforcement was made in error — reference the specific policy section and how your content complied. Submit all documents in English. TikTok may request additional information during review.

**Step 4 — Handle commission freezes explicitly.** If commissions are frozen for a policy violation, the same 30-day window applies. View frozen or deducted amounts under TikTok Studio → Tools → Monetization → Balance → Earning History (violation line items). If the appeal is approved, the freeze may be lifted. If you miss the window or the appeal is denied, frozen commissions **may be forfeited** — TikTok's policy uses "may," but the practical risk of permanent loss is high enough to treat the deadline as hard.

**Step 5 — Restore promotion access after content fixes.** Low CHR or Promotion Performance Score (PPS) can restrict which products you may promote even without an active violation ticket. Winning a content appeal removes the enforcement action and restores privileges tied to that case — but you may still need to rebuild CHR through compliant posts. Product eligibility rules live in TikTok's Affiliate Creator Product Selection Policy, linked from the Creator Enforcement doc.

Creators associated with an agency should note that commission splits may be blocked during a freeze on the creator account, not just the individual balance line you see in Studio.

## Violation types: risky behavior vs content vs performance

Not every violation wants the same evidence. Grouping your ticket correctly before you write a word saves rejections.

| Category | Typical triggers | Enforcement examples | Appeal strategy |
|----------|------------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Risky behavior** | Fraud, deception, fake engagement, multi-account evasion, illegal activity | Commission permanently withheld; account suspension; legal reporting | Extremely difficult; requires third-party logs, identity verification, and clean transaction trails — many cases have limited appeal rights |
| **Content & promotion** | Misleading claims, missing #ad disclosure, unoriginal video, medical/weight claims, low-quality shoppable content | Video removal, shoppable limits, CHR deductions, tagging restrictions | Policy citation + corrected content or proof claims are substantiated (COA, lab reports, manufacturer letters) |
| **Performance & fulfillment** (sellers) | Late dispatch, seller-fault cancellations, after-sales delays | OVL cuts, settlement delays, AHR deductions | Timestamped carrier acceptance scans, WMS/3PL logs, Seller Center screenshots with metadata |
| **Listing & catalog** (sellers) | Wrong category, IP complaints, duplicate listings, product authenticity | Listing freeze, campaign suspension | Correct path first; invoices matching shop entity, authenticity certificates, brand authorization |
| **Connected-account** | Shared bank or contact info with a penalized shop | Cross-account restrictions | Resolve root shop violation first; document account separation if legitimately distinct |

Risky-behavior buckets deserve extra caution. TikTok may permanently withhold commissions when enforcement cites deceptive, abusive, fraudulent, or illegal activity — and fund freezes during investigation can extend settlement from the standard 15 days after delivery to 31 days or longer while disputes resolve. Content violations, by contrast, are often winnable if you can show the video complied with disclosure rules or that a product claim matches supplier documentation.

Performance violations overlap with AHR deductions but still arrive as individual tickets with their own Correct/Appeal timers. Fixing late shipment at the metric level helps your score over time; winning the ticket appeal removes the enforcement action now.

## Evidence checklist: what TikTok actually accepts

TikTok's Violation & Appeal Guide publishes explicit documentation standards for seller appeals — especially identity and supply-chain cases. Treat this as a checklist, not a suggestion list.

**Required for many seller identity and authenticity appeals:**

- Clear photo of business license plus government-issued ID (front and back)
- Proof of warehouse ownership or lease (property documents, utility bills)
- Purchase contracts and invoices: business entity on documents must match shop registration; invoices should align with top-selling SKUs with visible price and quantity
- Required video: you holding business license and ID, face visible, no hats or glasses obscuring features

**Strong optional additions:**

- Timestamped carrier receipts showing acceptance scans before dispatch deadlines
- Unedited Seller Center screenshots (metadata intact — edited files are flagged)
- Third-party connector logs from ShipStation, Shopify, or your WMS
- Stock photos or warehouse walk-through video showing inventory

**For creator content appeals:**

- Side-by-side policy citation and frame-by-frame explanation of compliant elements
- Manufacturer documentation or third-party test results supporting product claims
- Proof of disclosure settings enabled for sponsored or gifted product content
- AI-generated or synthetic voice content labeled per current policy

**Automatic rejection patterns** documented by TikTok and reported in seller guidance (as of August 2026): incomplete packages, altered or cropped documents even to hide sensitive fields, emotional appeals without evidence, template letters copied from forums, and second appeals that recycle the same files without new proof.

Build the appeal around one narrative: "Here is the policy line, here is my record, here is why they match." Save PDFs and images as originals from the source system — not phone photos of a laptop screen.

## Recovering from commission restrictions and account limits

Winning an appeal removes the enforcement tied to that violation and restores associated privileges — but recovery has layers affiliates should plan for.

**Commission freeze lift.** Approved creator appeals may release frozen commissions. Until then, assume deferred settlement rules apply: TikTok may delay payout while security or policy checks run, especially if the account carries temporary restrictions. Check Earning History weekly rather than assuming a missed payout is a bank issue.

**Shoppable video frequency limits.** Policy violations can cap how many shoppable videos you post per period. Limits reset per the timeframe shown in the Manage Videos pop-up. Posted, scheduled, and even deleted shoppable videos count toward the cap; drafts do not. Compliance over the reset window is the recovery mechanism — there is no shortcut through a second appeal on the same ticket.

**Product promotion restrictions from low CHR or PPS.** Separate from a single violation appeal: if high-commission SKUs grey out in the product picker, check CHR and PPS thresholds before filing a dispute you cannot win. Recovery is posting compliant content that earns points back, not arguing eligibility on a closed ticket.

**Seller-side fund holds.** Standard violations may withhold balance for 45, 90, or 365 days; deceptive or fraudulent classifications can withhold permanently including platform incentives from the past 365 days. Successful appeals remove enforcement actions tied to the violation — fund release timing may still follow settlement policy, so track Seller Center finance messages alongside violation status.

**Account access restoration.** Temporary restrictions (tagging blocks, campaign suspensions, reduced visibility) typically clear when the appeal approves or the restriction period ends. Permanent removal for severe or repeated violations may not offer a standard appeal path — the Creator Enforcement Policy notes immediate enforcement for severe cases including account removal.

If both seller appeals fail, the escalation letter template in the Violation & Appeal Guide requires a root-cause analysis and rectification plan even when you believe the judgment was wrong — Scenario A disputes the facts; Scenario B acknowledges fault and documents prevention. Pick one story and evidence stack; mixed messages slow review.

## Conclusion

A TikTok Shop violation appeal is a timed procedure with different rules for sellers and creators — not a generic "contact support" problem. Sellers should check Violation Records the day a notice lands, use Correction when it is still available, and file the first Appeal within 30 calendar days with original documents. Creators get one shot per violation and must treat commission-freeze deadlines as seriously as account bans — milestone enforcement and repeat six-in-90-day strikes may fall outside appeal entirely.

Separate the ticket from your health score: winning an appeal can restore AHR or CHR points immediately, while ignoring a violation because your score "looks fine" can still cost you listings, payouts, or product access. Build evidence by violation category — risky behavior, content, and performance each demand different proof — and route stalled cases through official Seller Center or Studio support with the violation ID in hand.

The sellers and affiliates who recover fastest treat every enforcement email as a calendar event, not background noise. Read the ticket, pick Correct or Appeal before the timer expires, and keep your documentation chain clean enough that a reviewer who has never seen your shop can follow it in one pass.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I have to appeal a TikTok Shop violation?

US sellers have 30 calendar days from notification for a first appeal and 15 calendar days after a rejection for a second and final appeal. Some seller violations with delayed enforcement require appeal within 12–72 hours to prevent the penalty from applying. US creators have 30 calendar days from the enforcement date and only one appeal per violation, per TikTok's Creator Enforcement Policy as of July 2026.

### Can I appeal a TikTok Shop Account Health Rating milestone penalty?

Milestone enforcement actions on the creator side are explicitly not appealable through the standard violation ticket flow. Seller AHR milestone restrictions at 150, 100, 50, and 0 points are score-tier enforcements — you recover primarily through compliant operations, policy quizzes, order fulfillment, and successful appeals on individual violation tickets that caused deductions. See our Account Health Rating guide for score mechanics; use this article for ticket-level appeals.

### What evidence works best for a TikTok Shop appeal?

Original, unedited documents: timestamped shipping receipts, carrier acceptance scans, matching invoices and business licenses, unmodified Seller Center screenshots, and — for identity cases — the required video holding your license and ID. Creator content appeals need policy-specific proof such as disclosure settings, substantiation for product claims, or explanation of why content complies. Incomplete or altered files are automatically rejected per TikTok's Violation & Appeal Guide.

### What happens to my commissions during a violation appeal?

TikTok may freeze creator commissions while reviewing policy violations. You have 30 days to appeal; if approved, the freeze may lift. If the appeal is denied or not filed on time, frozen commissions may be forfeited. Deceptive, fraudulent, or illegal activity classifications carry the highest risk of permanent withholding. Agency commission splits may also be blocked during a creator freeze.

### What if both of my seller appeals are rejected?

After two rejected seller appeals, TikTok treats the violation decision as final for that case. You may seek escalation through an assigned account manager or the TTS US Seller Growth Community linked from the Violation & Appeal Guide — with a completed appeal letter template and screenshots, not screenshots alone. This does not guarantee reversal but is the documented escalation path for unmanaged sellers.

### Is a TikTok Shop violation the same as a low performance score?

No. Violation tickets are discrete enforcement events with specific penalties and appeal windows. Account Health Rating (sellers) and Creator Health Rating (creators) are rolling scores that track compliance over time. The same underlying issue may affect both, but fixing one does not automatically fix the other — appeal the ticket, then manage the score separately.
