## TL;DR

Search "TikTok Shop affiliate commission rates" and most answers quote a single percentage — 10%, 15%, 20% — as if that number lands in your bank account unchanged. It does not. US TikTok Shop affiliates earn through seller-set commission offers that differ by collaboration type (Open Plan vs Target Plan), apply only to attributed orders, and get clawed back when buyers return products before settlement closes. Typical Open Plan rates in the marketplace sit around 10%–15%; Target Plan deals for proven creators often run 18%–25%, with top-tier negotiations above that. This article helps US affiliates decide which offers are worth promoting — not to promote any single tool. Where Moras appears, we've noted it explicitly. This article was written by Kostja, based on analysis of US TikTok Shop affiliate workflows and creator economics data as of September 2026.

- **Open Plan**: public rate any approved creator can promote — usually 10%–15%, with category floors rising on many hardgoods SKUs
- **Target Plan**: seller invite with a custom rate that overrides Open for that creator and product — where higher earners negotiate
- **Attribution**: commission applies when a viewer buys through your tagged content within the attribution window — typically 7 days
- **Net math**: payout = (order revenue minus refunds) × rate, after delivery and the return window — budget on deposits, not dashboard estimates
- **Returns**: commission reverses on refunded orders; high-return SKUs can erase 10%–20% of headline earnings

## Why headline commission rates mislead affiliates

The affiliate marketplace shows you a percentage and a dollar amount per sale. A $38 kitchen gadget at 15% displays $5.70 in estimated commission. That number is useful for sorting products. It is not your income.

Three layers sit between the headline rate and what deposits. First, not every view converts, and not every conversion attributes to you — another creator's video, a Shop Tab browse, or a retargeted ad can take credit depending on the buyer's path. Second, TikTok holds commission until the order delivers and the return window closes, which typically adds 8 to 15 days after purchase, per the payout mechanics described in our [TikTok Shop setup guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-setup). Third, returns claw back commission on refunded orders, and category return rates of 5%–15% are common enough that treating gross commission as cash is how affiliates end up $600 short on a $2,000 dashboard month.

This confusion is structural, not accidental. Sellers think in margin stacks — referral fee, fulfillment, affiliate payout — which our [TikTok Shop fees guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-fees) maps from the seller side. Creators see only the affiliate slice. Both numbers are real; they describe opposite sides of the same transaction. A 15% commission on a $40 sale is $6.00 to you and a variable cost line to the seller who set it. Understanding that separation keeps you from chasing rates that look generous but attach to products with 20% return rates, or from dismissing a 12% Open Plan SKU that converts reliably while a 25% Target offer never ships samples.

The practical shift is to think in net commission per promoted SKU, not headline rate per product card. Rate × conversion × (1 − return rate) × attribution reliability is the formula that decides whether a side hustle clears $300 a month or stalls at $40. Everything below builds that model piece by piece.

## Open Plan vs Target Plan: the two commission systems

TikTok Shop US uses two primary collaboration types for standard affiliate commissions, per [TikTok Shop Seller Center documentation on affiliate collaborations](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837873164896001&lang=en), as of July 2026. Creators experience them as Open Plan offers visible in the Affiliate marketplace and Target Plan invitations sent by individual sellers.

**Open Collaboration (Open Plan)** makes selected products visible to the broad creator pool. Any eligible affiliate can add the product to a showcase and promote it at the published rate. Sellers set one standard commission per product, product group, or shop — technically anywhere from 1% to 80%, though marketplace listings cluster far lower. Open Plan is built for scale: sellers want volume content from many creators; affiliates get self-serve access without negotiation.

**Target Collaboration (Target Plan)** is an invite-only deal between one seller and specific creators for selected products. The seller sets a custom standard commission — again, anywhere from 1% to 80% on paper — plus optional Shop Ads commission tiers and sample terms. When the same creator promotes the same product under both Open and Target, **the Target rate always wins**, regardless of which is higher, according to Seller Center's commission priority rules.

The distinction matters for income planning. Open Plan is how you start: browse the marketplace, filter by commission, add products, post tagged videos. Target Plan is how you scale: once a seller sees you driving orders, they can invite you to a higher rate, free samples, or LIVE flash-sale terms to keep you from promoting a competitor's SKU. Industry guides commonly recommend sellers set Target rates a few points above Open — often 2 to 5 percentage points — for creators they want to retain, per [Hubfluence's commission breakdown](https://www.hubfluence.io/blog/tiktok-shop-commission-rates).

A third mode, **Shop Collaboration**, applies one universal rate across a qualified shop's eligible products. It behaves like a shop-wide Open Plan. You will see it less often as a new affiliate, but the priority rule is the same: a Target deal on a specific product still overrides the shop rate for that creator.

| Plan type | Who can access | Rate setting | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open Plan | All eligible affiliates (seller may require approval) | One public rate per product or group | Cold start, product testing, volume niches |
| Target Plan | Invited creators only | Custom rate per creator and product | Retention, hero SKUs, higher per-video economics |
| Shop Collaboration | Broad pool after seller approval | One rate across eligible shop products | Sellers with unified catalogs |

If you are still choosing between the affiliate path and running your own store, the economic split — inventory and referral fees versus commission-only income — is covered in our [two-path setup guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-setup). This article assumes you are already on the affiliate side and optimizing what you keep from each attributed order.

## What typical commission rates look like in 2026

There is no platform-wide "TikTok Shop affiliate rate." Sellers set commissions; the marketplace displays what they chose. Still, patterns are stable enough to plan around if you treat them as ranges, not guarantees.

**Open Plan marketplace listings** for US hardgoods and beauty commonly show **10%–15%** as the baseline band affiliates encounter when browsing without a Target invite, consistent with reporting from [ShortForm Nation's 2026 affiliate guide](https://www.shortformnation.com/blog/tiktok-shop-affiliate-marketing-the-complete-2026-guide) and seller-side summaries in [TikAdSuite's fee breakdown](https://tikadsuite.com/blog/tiktok-shop-fees/). Lower-ticket accessories and crowded categories sometimes list **5%–8%**; competitive beauty, wellness, and home SKUs often list **15%–20%** when sellers are buying discovery.

**Target Plan negotiations** for creators with demonstrated conversion — not just follower count — frequently land in the **18%–25%** band for mid-tier performers, with top creators in high-LTV categories reporting **25%–50%** on hero products, per the same ShortForm Nation tier framework. Those upper numbers are not entry-level offers. They attach to creators who already proved GMV, content quality, or LIVE conversion for that seller.

**Category floors are moving.** TikTok Shop pushed a US commission-structure update in August 2026 that raises the minimum Open Collaboration floor from 5% to **8% on many hardgoods categories** — home, beauty tools, fitness, kitchen, and similar — with full effect reported for **September 1, 2026**, per [Ecommerce Times' reporting on the affiliate commission restructure](https://ecommerce-times.com/tiktok-shops-u-s-affiliate-commission-restructure-is-shaking-dtc-brands/). Sellers above certain monthly GMV thresholds also face a Tier Bonus Pool contribution that funds incremental payouts to top creators. As an affiliate, you may see slightly higher Open rates on affected SKUs — but sellers under margin pressure may also tighten samples, raise prices, or shift marginal products off Open Collaboration entirely.

| Tier | Typical rate band | How you get it |
|---|---|---|
| Open — low competition SKU | 5%–10% | Marketplace browse; verify return rate before committing |
| Open — standard US listing | 10%–15% | Default for many health, home, and gadget categories |
| Open — aggressive discovery | 15%–20% | Seller buying content volume; often pairs with high return risk |
| Target — proven converter | 18%–25% | Seller invite after you drive attributed orders |
| Target — top-tier / LIVE anchor | 25%–50% | Negotiated; usually tied to volume or exclusivity expectations |

Always read the product card at promotion time. Sellers can edit Open rates; Target invites expire. Commission edits on Open plans can take up to **30 days** to apply to new promotions, while Target edits can take **5 days**, per Seller Center — another reason the rate on yesterday's screenshot may not match today's offer.

## How attribution decides whether you get paid

Commission rates are irrelevant if the order does not attribute to your content. TikTok Shop affiliate attribution ties a purchase to the creator whose shoppable link or product tag the buyer used within a defined window.

When a viewer taps your product tag — the shopping bag on your video or LIVE — TikTok records that touchpoint. If the viewer completes a purchase within the **attribution window**, typically **7 days** from that tap for standard US affiliate flows, the order counts toward your commission at the active rate for that product and collaboration type. This window is why "inspired" viewers who remember your demo but buy later through search may not credit you unless they use your link path.

Attribution is last-touch within the affiliate ecosystem, with Target rates overriding Open rates when both apply to you and the product. Shop Ads add a wrinkle: if a seller boosts your video with Video Shopping Ads, you still earn the standard or Shop Ads commission rate set in your collaboration — ads spend is the seller's cost, not a separate creator payout, per Seller Center's ads commission priority table.

Common attribution failures affiliates misread as "TikTok didn't pay me":

- The buyer purchased outside the 7-day window
- The buyer clicked another creator's tag after yours
- The product was purchased from the Shop Tab without your tag path
- You promoted a SKU before the Target invite was accepted, so the lower Open rate applied
- The order was canceled before shipment — no settled commission

The fix is procedural. Use product tags on every shoppable post, pin the tagged product in LIVE, and check Affiliate Center for **estimated versus settled** commission on each SKU. Estimated commission is a forecast; settled commission is what survived delivery, returns, and policy checks.

## The payout timeline and return clawbacks

Dashboard commission and bank deposit are separated by a delay that catches new affiliates every month. When an attributed order places, TikTok records estimated commission immediately. Payout waits until the order **delivers** and the **return window closes** — often **8 to 15 days** after purchase, depending on category and shipping speed, as outlined in our setup guide's commission timeline section.

During that hold, buyers can return items. TikTok Shop's buyer-side standard return window is **30 days** on many US orders, with separate Money-Back Guarantee rules for damaged or defective items. If a buyer returns a product before your commission settles, the platform **reverses** the affiliate commission on that order. Sellers face their own refund fee stack; you lose the entire commission line — there is no partial clawback on a fully returned unit.

That reversal is the "clawback" affiliates feel in real bank accounts. Promote a beauty SKU with a 15% return rate and $1,000 in estimated commission, and **$150** may disappear in refunds before payout — before counting chargebacks or partial refunds on multi-item orders. Apparel, beauty, and electronics skew high; consumables and low-return home goods skew lower. The product analytics panel in Affiliate Center often surfaces return signals; experienced affiliates treat double-digit return rates as a filter, not a footnote.

Settlement formula, per TikTok's affiliate policy framing cited in industry summaries: **Commission = (Revenue − Refunds) × Commission rate**, calculated on the order value eligible for affiliate payout before the commission is finalized. Partial refunds reduce commission proportionally; full returns zero it out.

> The affiliate who budgets from estimated commission is planning with someone else's money. The affiliate who budgets from settled commission after a 90-day return-rate average on each hero SKU is running a business.

For a broader picture of how return clawbacks interact with conversion troubleshooting, see the return-rate bottleneck in our [no-sales diagnosis guide](/blog/tiktok-shop-no-sales). The mechanics are the same whether sales are zero or commission is inflated by refunds.

## Net commission math — worked examples with returns

The tables below are illustrative planning models, not income guarantees. Substitute your own AOV, rate, and return assumptions from Affiliate Center.

### Example 1: Single order, no return

| Line item | Value |
|---|---|
| Product price | $42.00 |
| Open Plan commission rate | 15% |
| Gross commission | $6.30 |
| Return | None |
| **Net commission to you** | **$6.30** |

Simple case — useful for sanity-checking product cards. Most months are not one order.

### Example 2: Ten orders, category-average returns

Assume you drive **10 attributed orders** on a $42 gadget at **15% Open Plan**, with a **12% return rate** (one return, one partial return scenario simplified to 12% revenue clawback):

| Line item | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gross order revenue attributed | 10 × $42 | $420.00 |
| Refunded revenue | 12% × $420 | $50.40 |
| Net eligible revenue | $420 − $50.40 | $369.60 |
| Commission at 15% | $369.60 × 0.15 | **$55.44** |
| Headline commission if you ignored returns | $420 × 0.15 | $63.00 |
| **Return drag** | | **−$7.56 (−12%)** |

You would have over-budgeted by $7.56 on this batch — about **12%** — by using gross instead of net. Scale that to $3,000 estimated monthly commission and a 12% return rate, and the gap exceeds **$360**.

### Example 3: Open Plan vs Target Plan on the same SKU

Same $42 AOV, **10% return rate**, **10 orders**:

| Plan | Rate | Net revenue | Net commission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open Plan | 12% | $378.00 | $45.36 |
| Target Plan | 22% | $378.00 | $83.16 |
| **Target uplift** | +10 pts | — | **+$37.80 (+83%)** |

A 10-point Target deal nearly doubles net commission at the same conversion and return profile. That is why side-hustle affiliates topping out on Open rates hit a volume ceiling — and why our [90-day side-hustle timeline](/blog/tiktok-affiliate-side-hustle) recommends pursuing Target relationships only after you have conversion proof, not on day one.

### Example 4: High rate, toxic returns — when 20% pays less than 12%

| SKU | Rate | Return rate | Net commission on $420 gross (10 orders × $42) |
|---|---|---|---|
| "High commission" beauty serum | 20% | 22% | $65.52 |
| "Lower commission" kitchen tool | 12% | 6% | $47.09 |
| **Better hero SKU** | 12% | 6% | — |

Wait — the high-rate SKU still wins in gross terms. Now add **half the conversion rate** because the serum's claims attract skeptical buyers who return more often, and your video volume drops:

If the serum drives only **6 orders** instead of 10 at 22% returns:

- Net revenue: 6 × $42 × 0.78 = $196.56  
- Commission at 20%: **$39.31**

The kitchen tool at 12% with 10 orders and 6% returns still delivers **$47.09**. **Higher headline rate lost.**

The lesson: optimize net commission per hour of content, not rate per tap in the marketplace. Return rate and conversion belong in the same spreadsheet as commission percentage.

## When to negotiate a Target Plan deal

Target Plan is not something you apply for on day three. It is a seller's retention tool. You earn leverage by showing attributed GMV, content reliability, and a return profile that does not destroy the seller's margin.

**Signals that you are ready to ask:**

- You drove **5+ settled orders** on the seller's SKU in 30 days, not just views
- Your return rate on that SKU is **below category average** — you demonstrate the product in a way buyers keep
- You post consistently (3+ tagged videos per week) so the seller risks losing shelf space in your showcase
- Open Plan rate caps your earnings below your hourly floor — common when Open sits at 10%–12% on a $35 AOV

**How to approach it:** Message the seller through TikTok's collaboration tools or the contact line on their Target invite template (email / WhatsApp fields sellers often include, per Seller Center). Lead with data: attributed orders, content links, average return rate if favorable. Ask for a **Target Collaboration** at a defined rate — industry practice is **Open rate + 3 to 8 points**, depending on your conversion — plus sample flow if you are self-funding purchases today.

**When to walk away:** The seller wants Target exclusivity without a rate premium, raises rates verbally but never sends the invite, or the SKU's return rate climbs above **15%** even with good content. A Target Plan at 18% on a 20%-return product is still a clawback trap.

**Timing note:** With Open floor increases rolling out in September 2026, proactive sellers may invite top converters early to lock rates before marketplace repricing. If you are in that cohort, respond quickly — Target invites expire, and duplicate collaborations can be reused by sellers for new creator batches.

## What to avoid when reading commission numbers

**Chasing the highest rate in the marketplace.** High Open rates often compensate for weak product-market fit, thin reviews, or return-prone categories. Filter return signals first.

**Treating estimated commission as spendable cash.** Budget on settled payouts after your typical return lag, especially in the first 90 days when you are still learning category norms — the same budgeting discipline our [side-hustle guide](/blog/tiktok-affiliate-side-hustle) applies to part-time affiliates.

**Promoting before you accept a Target invite.** Until accepted, Open terms apply. Screenshot the invite rate and acceptance timestamp for your records.

**Ignoring attribution mechanics in LIVE.** Flash-sale Target collaborations can require LIVE-capable creators and single-creator windows, per Seller Center. A short-video-only workflow will not capture those deals.

**Confusing seller fees with your payout.** The 8% referral fee sellers pay TikTok is not deducted from your commission — it is parallel economics. Do not subtract it mentally unless you are also selling as a merchant; sellers model that in our [fees breakdown](/blog/tiktok-shop-fees).

**Flat-fee video deals mixed with commission.** Some Target offers include a flat fee for one shoppable video plus optional sales commission. That is a hybrid brand deal, not pure affiliate math — amortize the flat fee across expected orders before comparing to Open-only SKUs.

## Conclusion

TikTok Shop affiliate commissions in 2026 are seller-set, plan-dependent, and net of returns — not a single platform rate. Open Plan gets you in the door at typical marketplace bands of **10%–15%**, with category floors moving toward **8%+** on many hardgoods SKUs as of September 2026. Target Plan is where experienced affiliates raise effective earnings per video by negotiating **18%–25%** or higher on SKUs they already convert, because Target rates override Open rates for the same creator and product.

The math that matters is attribution × (rate on net revenue after refunds) × conversion — not the headline percentage on the product card. Hold commission estimates until delivery and return windows close, model return drag honestly, and push for Target deals only when your data proves you are worth retaining. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the average TikTok Shop affiliate commission rate in 2026?

There is no fixed US average — sellers set rates. Open Plan listings commonly cluster around 10%–15%; Target Plan deals for proven creators often run 18%–25%, with top-tier negotiations higher. TikTok allows commissions from 1% to 80% per Seller Center rules, but marketplace reality concentrates far below the ceiling. Check the active offer on each product at promotion time.

### What is the difference between Open Plan and Target Plan?

Open Plan is a public commission any eligible affiliate can promote in the marketplace. Target Plan is an invite-only deal with a custom rate for specific creators and products. If both apply to you on the same SKU, the Target rate always pays. Target is how sellers retain top converters; Open is how most affiliates start.

### When do TikTok Shop affiliates get paid?

Commission is estimated when an attributed order places, but payout typically waits until the order delivers and the return window closes — often 8 to 15 days after purchase, plus processing time. If the buyer returns the item before settlement, commission is reversed. Plan cash flow around settled payouts, not dashboard estimates.

### Do affiliates lose commission on returns?

Yes. Full returns zero out affiliate commission on that order. Partial refunds reduce commission proportionally. High-return categories can claw back 10%–20% of headline monthly commission. Factor return rate into product selection before you optimize video hooks.

### How does the TikTok Shop affiliate attribution window work?

When a viewer taps your product tag and buys within the attribution window — typically 7 days in standard US flows — the order credits your commission at the active Open or Target rate. Purchases outside that window or through another creator's tag may not attribute to you, even if your content influenced the buyer.

### How do I get a higher commission rate on TikTok Shop?

Drive settled attributed orders on a seller's SKU, keep return rates reasonable, and post consistently. Then request a Target Collaboration at a premium above the Open rate — commonly a few percentage points, negotiated per seller. Cold DMs without conversion proof rarely work; data and content links do.

### Did TikTok Shop raise minimum affiliate commission rates in 2026?

Yes, for many US hardgoods categories. Reporting on an August 2026 Seller Center update describes the Open Collaboration minimum floor rising from 5% to 8% on categories such as home, beauty tools, fitness, and kitchen, with changes taking effect September 1, 2026. Individual product cards remain the source of truth for any SKU you promote.
