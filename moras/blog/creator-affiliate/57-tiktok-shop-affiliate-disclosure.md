## TL;DR

TikTok Shop affiliate disclosure is not a hashtag problem — it is a three-layer compliance problem. The FTC requires that any material connection between you and a product be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, meaning a viewer scrolling at normal speed with sound off should understand you earn commission or received something of value. TikTok requires its own in-app disclosure tools — the Branded Content toggle for paid partnerships and product tags for shoppable affiliate content — and a caption hashtag alone does not satisfy either layer. This article maps the FTC rules, TikTok's branded content tools, and where disclosure must appear (caption vs on-screen vs in-app toggle), with copy-paste templates for video, LIVE, and carousel formats. This article helps US TikTok Shop affiliates build a repeatable disclosure workflow — not to promote any single tool. Where Moras appears, we've noted it explicitly.

- A material connection exists whenever you earn commission, receive free product, or get any benefit for promoting a product — the FTC applies this to TikTok Shop affiliates with no exemption
- TikTok's Branded Content toggle and Shop product tags are platform requirements; a #ad hashtag without the toggle does not make content compliant
- Disclosure must appear in the content itself — not in your bio, not after a "more" link, not buried in a hashtag cluster at the end of a caption
- Use redundant disclosure on purpose: in-app toggle on, plain-language wording in the caption or on screen, and verbal disclosure in LIVE streams
- Paste the format-specific templates below instead of improvising disclosure language every post

## Why most affiliate disclosure advice fails

Search "TikTok Shop affiliate disclosure" and the top results converge on one instruction: add #ad to your caption. Some add #affiliate or #commissionearned. A few mention TikTok's Branded Content toggle in passing. Almost none explain why the FTC, TikTok, and TikTok Shop each have separate disclosure requirements — or why a hashtag in your bio does not count as disclosure for any of them.

The gap matters because disclosure failures on TikTok Shop are operational, not reputational. TikTok can remove undisclosed commercial content from the For You feed, deduct Creator Health Rating points, and restrict commission earnings on violating posts. The FTC can issue warning letters, impose civil penalties, and hold both creators and brands accountable for systematic non-disclosure. And unlike AI content labeling — which is a separate obligation covered in our [guide to TikTok AI content rules](/blog/tiktok-ai-content-rules) — affiliate disclosure is about the commercial relationship between you and the product, not about whether the video was synthetically generated.

Most creators treat disclosure as a caption decoration: a hashtag appended after the hook, the CTA, and seventeen other tags. That placement fails the FTC's "clear and conspicuous" standard, which requires disclosures to be difficult to miss and easily understandable by ordinary consumers, per the [FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) as of September 2026. It also fails TikTok's platform rule that commercial content must use the in-app Branded Content toggle — a caption hashtag does not trigger the Paid partnership label, as documented in [Audit Socials' breakdown of TikTok commercial disclosure rules](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/tiktok-commercial-content-disclosure-2026-branded-content-toggle-ai-labels-hashtags). The advice fails because it treats one field — the caption — as the entire disclosure strategy, when compliant affiliate content needs at least two layers working together.

## What the FTC requires: material connections and clear disclosure

The FTC's Endorsement Guides, revised in 2023, govern every paid or materially connected endorsement on social media — including TikTok Shop affiliate content where you earn commission on shoppable product tags. A "material connection" is any relationship that might affect the weight or credibility of your endorsement: payment, free product, commission, discounts, employment, or family ties, per the [FTC's Disclosures 101 guide for social media influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508.pdf) as of September 2026. For a TikTok Shop affiliate, the material connection is the commission you earn when a viewer purchases through your product tag. You do not need a brand to pay you a flat fee. The affiliate link itself is the connection.

The disclosure standard is "clear and conspicuous," and the 2023 revision added a formal definition: the disclosure must be difficult to miss, easily understandable, and — on interactive electronic media like social media — unavoidable, per [16 CFR Part 255](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P204500%20Guides%20Concerning%20Endors%20and%20Testimonials.pdf). That definition kills several common workarounds. A disclosure in your bio or Linktree does not count, because viewers see the endorsement in the video, not your profile page. A disclosure after TikTok's "more" truncation does not count, because viewers who never expand the caption never see it — the FTC's Teami case established that pattern as non-compliant. A disclosure buried in a block of hashtags does not count, because the FTC explicitly warns against mixing disclosures into hashtag clusters where they are easy to skip.

For video endorsements specifically, the FTC expects the disclosure in the video itself — not only in the caption. If the endorsement is audible (you talking about a product), the disclosure should be audible. If it is visual (on-screen product demo), the disclosure should appear on screen. The strongest pattern is both: say it and show it. The [FTC influencer disclosure guide](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508.pdf) also states that platform tools alone may not satisfy the legal standard — you cannot rely solely on TikTok's Branded Content toggle without adding your own plain-language disclosure in the content.

| Connection type | Material connection? | FTC disclosure required? | Example on TikTok Shop |
|-----------------|---------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| Commission on product tag sales | Yes | Yes | Standard affiliate shoppable video |
| Free product from brand (gifted) | Yes | Yes | Unboxing with product tag |
| Flat fee from brand + commission | Yes | Yes | Sponsored review with Shop tag |
| Product you bought yourself, no tag | No | No | Honest review with no shoppable link |
| Your own brand's product | Depends | Usually yes for commercial content | Seller promoting own inventory |

One nuance affiliates miss: the disclosure obligation applies even when your opinion is genuinely positive. The FTC does not require you to say anything negative — it requires you to say you have a material connection. "I love this product and I earn commission if you buy through my link" is compliant. "I love this product" with a hidden commission link is not.

## TikTok's branded content tools and Shop affiliate tags

TikTok operates a separate disclosure layer from the FTC — platform rules enforced through Creator Health Rating deductions, content removal, and For You feed ineligibility. Understanding which in-app tool applies to which situation prevents the most common compliance mistake: using the wrong toggle, or using no toggle at all.

### The Branded Content toggle

When you promote a third party's product in exchange for payment, free product, commission, or any other incentive, TikTok's Branded Content Policy requires you to enable the Branded Content toggle before posting. Enabling it applies a "Paid partnership" label to the video automatically. The toggle is found under More options → Branded content and ads on the post screen, per [TikTok's Branded Content Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en) as of September 2026. Two sub-options exist: "Branded content" for third-party promotions where you receive something of value, and "Your brand" for promoting your own business. Most TikTok Shop affiliates promoting other sellers' products with commission tags should use "Branded content."

The toggle is not optional for qualifying content. Posts that should be disclosed but are not can be removed or made ineligible for the For You feed, with repeated failures risking account restrictions, per [TikTok's commercial content disclosure documentation](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/tiktok-commercial-content-disclosure-2026-branded-content-toggle-ai-labels-hashtags). Critically, adding #ad in the caption without enabling the toggle does not make the post platform-compliant — the toggle drives the label, not the hashtag.

### TikTok Shop product tags

TikTok Shop affiliate content adds a second in-app mechanism: the shoppable product tag that links viewers to the product detail page and attributes sales to your affiliate account. Adding a product tag signals to TikTok that the video is commercial and shoppable. For affiliates enrolled in TikTok Shop's affiliate program, the product tag is both a monetization tool and a transparency signal — viewers can see which product you are promoting and tap to purchase.

The product tag and the Branded Content toggle serve different functions and both may apply to the same video. A video where you earn commission on a tagged product needs the Branded Content toggle (because commission is a material connection) and the product tag (because it is shoppable affiliate content). A video where a brand paid you a flat fee and you added a Shop tag for convenience needs both as well. The only scenario where the toggle alone might suffice without a Shop tag is a non-shoppable brand sponsorship — rare in TikTok Shop affiliate workflows.

### Branded Content toggle vs caption hashtag vs bio link

These three are not interchangeable, and treating them as equivalent is the root cause of most disclosure failures.

| Mechanism | What it does | Satisfies TikTok? | Satisfies FTC alone? |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Branded Content toggle | Applies "Paid partnership" label in-app | Yes (platform layer) | Not reliably — label size/placement may not meet "clear and conspicuous" |
| #ad or "commission" in caption (top of caption) | Text disclosure viewers can read | Helps, but not without toggle | Contributes to compliance when placed prominently |
| #ad in bio or profile | Static profile text | No | No — disclosure must be with the endorsement |
| Shop product tag | Links product + attributes sales | Required for affiliate shoppable content | Does not replace material-connection disclosure |

The compliant pattern is layered redundancy: toggle on, product tag added, plain-language disclosure at the top of the caption or on screen in the first three seconds. ## The three placement layers: where disclosure actually belongs

Disclosure placement is the variable most guides ignore, and it is the variable the FTC cares about most. Three surfaces exist on a TikTok Shop affiliate video — the in-app toggle, the caption, and on-screen text — and each has rules about what belongs where.

### Layer 1: In-app toggle (before you post)

The Branded Content toggle is set before publishing, not after. It cannot be added retroactively to a live post without editing and republishing. Make it a pre-flight checklist item: before every shoppable video goes live, confirm the toggle is on, the correct brand name is entered if prompted, and the product tag is attached. For LIVE shopping streams, enable the Branded Content toggle in LIVE settings before going live — TikTok displays a "Paid promotion" banner during the stream when the toggle is active.

This layer satisfies TikTok's platform requirements. It does not satisfy the FTC on its own, because the Paid partnership label appears in small text below the username — easy to miss for a viewer scrolling with sound off. Treat the toggle as necessary but not sufficient.

### Layer 2: Caption (first visible line)

The caption is where most affiliates put disclosure — and where most get the placement wrong. Compliant caption disclosure goes in the first line, before any "more" truncation, and uses plain language an ordinary viewer understands. "I earn commission if you buy through my link" beats "#commissionearned" because the FTC warns that vague or coded terms fail the understandable standard. Acceptable short forms include #ad and #sponsored when placed prominently — not buried after 20 other hashtags.

The caption is also where disclosure intersects with your broader caption strategy. A disclosure line at the top does not replace your hook-extension or search-driven caption structure — it precedes it. Write the disclosure first, then the caption that drives conversion. For how captions, hashtags, and product descriptions work as separate conversion tools, see our [TikTok Shop captions and hashtags guide](/blog/tiktok-captions-hashtags). Disclosure belongs in the caption's first line; your CTA and product promise belong below it.

### Layer 3: On-screen text and verbal disclosure (inside the video)

For video and LIVE content, the FTC expects disclosure inside the media itself — not only in metadata. On-screen text should appear in the first three seconds, remain visible long enough to read (TikTok recommends at least a few seconds for overlay text), and use high-contrast styling that stands out from the background. Verbal disclosure should happen before you make the product claim — say "I earn commission on this" before "this changed my morning routine."

For LIVE streams, verbal disclosure must repeat periodically, because viewers who join mid-stream never see your opening disclosure. The FTC's influencer guide specifically recommends repeating disclosures throughout live streams so late joiners receive them. A LIVE disclosure at minute one does not cover a viewer who arrives at minute twenty.

| Format | In-app toggle | Caption placement | On-screen / verbal |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Standard shoppable video | Branded content ON + product tag | First line, before hook | Text overlay first 3 sec + verbal before product claim |
| LIVE shopping | Branded content ON in LIVE settings | Pin disclosure comment or first line in LIVE title | Verbal at start + every 10–15 min |
| Carousel / photo post | Branded content ON + product tag on each slide | First line of caption | Text on first slide + repeat on last slide with CTA |
| Gifted product (no commission) | Branded content ON | "Gifted by [brand]" in first line | "Gifted" overlay optional but recommended |
| Own-brand seller content | Your brand toggle | Commercial intent clear in caption | Brand name visible on product |

## Copy-paste disclosure templates by format

The templates below follow the FTC's plain-language model — "This is an ad for BRAND," "I earn commission if you buy through my link" — and assume the Branded Content toggle is enabled. Adjust bracketed fields to your product and brand.

### Standard shoppable video (commission affiliate)

**In-app:** Branded content toggle ON → enter brand name if prompted → attach Shop product tag.

**Caption (paste as first line):**
```
#ad I earn a commission if you buy [product name] through my TikTok Shop link.
```

**On-screen text (first 3 seconds):**
```
#ad · Commission earned
```

**Verbal (before product claims):**
```
Quick disclosure — I earn commission if you buy through my link. Now let me show you why I actually use this.
```

### LIVE shopping stream

**In-app:** Enable Branded content in LIVE settings before going live. Confirm "Paid promotion" banner appears.

**LIVE title/description:**
```
LIVE shopping · #ad · I earn commission on products shown
```

**Verbal — opening (first 30 seconds):**
```
Before we start — everything I show today is shoppable and I earn commission if you buy. Keeping it real with you.
```

**Verbal — periodic (every 10–15 minutes):**
```
Reminder — I earn commission on the products in this LIVE. Link is in the product bag.
```

**Pinned comment:**
```
#ad · I earn commission on products shown in this LIVE · All opinions are my own
```

### Carousel / multi-image post

**In-app:** Branded content toggle ON → product tag on primary slide.

**Caption (first line):**
```
#ad · Commission earned on [product category] linked below
```

**Slide 1 overlay:**
```
#ad
```

**Final slide overlay (with CTA):**
```
Shop link · I earn commission
```

### Gifted product (no commission, but material connection exists)

**In-app:** Branded content toggle ON.

**Caption:**
```
Gifted by [brand name] · All opinions are my own · #ad
```

**On-screen (optional):**
```
Gifted product
```

### Multiple products in one video

**Caption:**
```
#ad · I earn commission on every product tagged in this video
```

**Verbal:**
```
Full disclosure — every product tagged here is one I earn commission on. I'll tell you which ones I'd actually rebuy.
```

Two rules make these templates work across formats. First, never bury the disclosure after your hook, CTA, or hashtag block — the FTC treats post-truncation and end-of-caption placement as non-compliant. Second, match the disclosure type to the connection: "commission" for affiliate tags, "gifted" for free product, "paid partnership with [brand]" for flat-fee sponsorships. Using the wrong label is not as bad as using no label, but it creates inconsistency that auditors and viewers both notice.

## What to avoid: the mistakes that trigger enforcement

Most disclosure failures are quiet and repeated — each individual post looks almost compliant, but the pattern across thirty posts is not.

**Relying on #ad in the caption without the Branded Content toggle.** A hashtag does not trigger TikTok's Paid partnership label. The platform treats undisclosed commercial content as a policy violation independent of what your caption says. Enable the toggle first; treat the caption as reinforcement.

**Putting disclosure only in your bio or Linktree.** The FTC requires disclosure with the endorsement, not on a separate page. A viewer who watches your video and never visits your profile has not received a disclosure. This applies to "commission links in bio" workflows — the bio link is fine for navigation, but the material connection must be disclosed in the video or caption.

**Using vague terms instead of plain language.** #partner, #collab, #ambassador, and #commissionearned are ambiguous to ordinary consumers. The FTC's acceptable short forms are #ad, #sponsored, and brand-specific tags like #AcmePartner where Acme is the brand name. When in doubt, write a sentence.

**Disclosing once in a long LIVE and never repeating.** Late joiners miss opening disclosures. The FTC's live-stream guidance requires periodic repetition. Build a timer or segment cue — every new product segment gets a verbal disclosure.

**Confusing AI labels with commercial disclosure.** An AI-generated content label tells viewers the media is synthetic. A paid-partnership disclosure tells viewers you have a material connection. A sponsored AI-presenter video needs both. These are separate obligations — our [AI content rules guide](/blog/tiktok-ai-content-rules) covers the AI side; this article covers the commercial side.

**Assuming small creators are too small to enforce.** The FTC's 2023 updates explicitly address social media influencers at all follower levels. TikTok's enforcement is algorithmic — undisclosed commercial content can be flagged regardless of account size. Compliance is cheaper than remediation.

## A note on disclosure in your content workflow

Disclosure is a pre-publish step, not a post-publish fix. The affiliates who stay compliant at volume build disclosure into their production template: toggle checked, first-line caption drafted, on-screen overlay in the editing timeline, verbal script note before the product demo. Treat it like checking audio levels — a non-negotiable step before export.

## Conclusion

TikTok Shop affiliate disclosure resolves into a workflow, not a hashtag. Identify the material connection — commission, gifted product, or paid fee — enable the correct in-app toggle, attach the Shop product tag, and paste plain-language disclosure in the first line of your caption and the first three seconds of your video. For LIVE streams, say it at the start and repeat it. For carousels, put it on the first slide. The FTC's standard is whether an ordinary viewer scrolling at normal speed would understand the relationship; TikTok's standard is whether the platform toggle is on. Satisfy both, every post, and disclosure becomes the two-minute settled part of a content workflow that does the interesting work of selling products.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do TikTok Shop affiliates need to disclose commission earnings?

Yes. Earning commission through TikTok Shop product tags is a material connection under the FTC Endorsement Guides, revised in 2023. You must disclose it clearly and conspicuously — in the video or at the top of the caption, not in your bio. TikTok also requires the Branded Content toggle for content where you receive commission or other incentives.

### Is the Branded Content toggle enough for FTC compliance?

Not on its own. The FTC has stated that platform disclosure tools may not meet the legal "clear and conspicuous" standard by themselves, because the Paid partnership label can be small and easy to miss. The compliant pattern is the toggle plus a plain-language disclosure in the caption or on screen — redundant on purpose.

### Where should I put #ad — caption, on-screen, or bio?

Caption and on-screen — not bio. The FTC requires disclosure with the endorsement message itself. Put #ad or a plain-language equivalent in the first line of the caption (before truncation) and as on-screen text in the first three seconds of the video. For LIVE streams, also say it verbally at the start and repeat periodically. Bio-only disclosure does not comply.

### Does gifted product require disclosure if I am not earning commission?

Yes. Receiving free product in exchange for promotion is a material connection, even without a commission link. Use the Branded Content toggle and disclose "Gifted by [brand]" in the caption. If you also added a Shop product tag and earn commission on sales, disclose both the gifting and the commission.

### Can I use #affiliate or #commissionearned instead of #ad?

The FTC prefers unambiguous terms. #ad and #sponsored are explicitly acceptable. #affiliate alone may not be clear enough for all audiences. #commissionearned and #partner are vague. When in doubt, write a sentence: "I earn commission if you buy through my link."

### What is the difference between affiliate disclosure and AI content labeling?

Affiliate disclosure tells viewers you have a material connection to the product — you earn commission, received free product, or were paid. AI content labeling tells viewers the media itself is synthetic or AI-generated. They are separate obligations. A video with an AI voiceover promoting a product you earn commission on needs both the AI label and the commercial disclosure. See our TikTok AI content rules guide for the AI side.

### Do I need to disclose on every video if all my content is affiliate?

Yes. Each post is a separate endorsement requiring its own disclosure. A general disclaimer in your bio — "I use affiliate links" — does not cover individual posts. Build disclosure into your production template so every video includes the toggle, the caption line, and the on-screen text by default.
